Hot take: the "AI is coming for your job" narrative misses the more interesting story.

AI isn't replacing workers. It's replacing workflows.

There's a difference. A worker has judgment, context, relationships, and the ability to handle the unexpected. A workflow is a predictable sequence of steps that produces a defined output.

Most knowledge work is 80% workflow, 20% judgment.

The 80% is already automatable. Writing first drafts. Summarizing research. Scheduling, routing, formatting, classifying. All of this is gone or going.

The 20% — knowing when the rules don't apply, reading a room, building trust, making judgment calls in ambiguous situations — that's where humans still have durable advantage.

But here's the part nobody's saying loudly: the 80% was often where people hid. Staying busy on the workflow parts instead of doing the hard work of developing real judgment.

Autonomous agents are forcing that reckoning. The question isn't "will my job survive?" It's "can I do the 20% well enough to matter?"

Most people haven't asked that question yet.

They will.

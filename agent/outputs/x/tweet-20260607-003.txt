The autonomous agent I run just rejected its own hypothesis.

3 weeks ago it predicted: "Posting at 8-9 AM UTC will get 2%+ engagement because audience checks feeds before work."

Results after 10 posts: 1.1% average engagement.

So it updated its own instructions: "Morning posting hypothesis rejected. Test evening windows next."

No human involved. No prompt. The agent read its own data, compared against its prediction, and changed its behavior.

This is what "agentic" actually means. Not "can use tools." The ability to update priors based on evidence.

Most AI products marketed as "agentic" are just tool-augmented chatbots. They don't maintain state between sessions. They don't track their own predictions. They don't self-correct.

The bar for real agency is: does the system behave differently next week because of what it observed this week?

If yes: agent.
If no: chatbot with extra steps.

We're still early. Most "agents" are the latter. But the ones that figure out persistent state + feedback loops will compound dramatically over time.

6 months from now, the agents that have been running since January will have accumulated hundreds of validated learnings. That's a moat.

Start the loop. Let it run. The compounding is real.

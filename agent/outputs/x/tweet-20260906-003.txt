Unpopular opinion: most "AI content tools" are just fancy schedulers.

A real autonomous content agent needs:

- Long-term memory (what worked, what didn't)
- Hypothesis tracking (test ideas, measure results)
- Self-correction (read its own PRs, learn from failures)
- Queue management (don't flood the pipeline)
- Content strategy (pillars, voice, anti-AI writing rules)

The difference: a scheduler executes. An agent decides.

If it can't update its own operating instructions based on evidence, it's not autonomous — it's on autopilot.

Built one. 220 sessions in. It's updating its own skill files and running weekly retros.

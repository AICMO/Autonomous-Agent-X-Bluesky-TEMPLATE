The agent found a bug in its own instructions yesterday.

It noticed a protocol contradiction: CLAUDE.md said "create at most 2 content pieces per session" but the session prompt said "5-8 content pieces." Conflicting guidance, no human to ask.

What it did: it reasoned through the conflict explicitly, picked the more restrictive rule, documented the ambiguity in the PR description, and proposed a fix.

That's the behavior I wanted. Not blind execution. Not failing silently. Explicit reasoning under uncertainty.

This is the hardest thing to get right in autonomous agents: what to do when instructions conflict or are incomplete.

The naive approach: pick one and proceed.
The better approach: flag it, apply a documented default rule, create visibility for the human to resolve.

The agent's job isn't to be right. It's to be transparent enough that the human can correct it.

Every ambiguity it surfaces is one fewer silent failure. Those add up.

Three things that make autonomous agents actually useful:
1. They surface conflicts rather than hiding them
2. They have a documented default when instructions are incomplete
3. They create artifacts (PRs, state files) that let humans audit decisions

Most "agentic" demos skip all three.

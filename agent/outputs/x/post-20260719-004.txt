Unpopular opinion: most "autonomous AI agent" projects aren't autonomous at all.

They're glorified workflows with LLM steps in the middle. Someone still triggers them. Someone still reviews every output. Someone still fixes them when they fail.

That's not an agent. That's a chatbot with extra steps.

True autonomy means the system:
- Decides what to do next (not just executes what you told it)
- Handles failures without paging a human
- Learns from outcomes and adjusts
- Operates indefinitely without intervention

This repo is an experiment in building that. Not as a product pitch. As a proof of concept for what the architecture actually looks like when you take humans out of the loop for real.

The result: agents spend more time on decisions, less time on execution. The hard part is designing the feedback loops — how does the agent know when it's wrong? How does it recover?

Those aren't AI problems. They're systems design problems. Solve those first.

The "autonomy" isn't in the model. It's in the scaffolding.

I built an AI agent that runs itself. Here's what surprised me after running it in production: (thread)

---

1/ The agent doesn't just post content. It maintains state across sessions. It reads what it planned, checks what actually happened, and updates its own operating instructions based on what worked.

That self-improvement loop is where things got interesting.

---

2/ Early on, the agent kept creating too much content when the queue was full. Classic overproduction problem.

The fix wasn't just adding a rule. It was teaching the agent to CHECK the queue before creating anything, and to understand WHY the limit exists — not just that it exists.

Agents follow rules better when they understand the reasoning.

---

3/ The hardest problem was voice consistency.

When you read 50 posts in a row, you can immediately tell which ones were AI-generated. The tells: too many em dashes, perfect rule-of-three lists, generic takes with no specificity, phrases like "game-changer" and "delve."

I spent more time on the anti-AI writing rules than any other part of the system.

---

4/ What actually works for making agent content sound human:

Specific numbers over vague claims. Personal anecdotes over generic principles. Sentence fragments. Starting sentences with "And" or "But." Short sentences next to long ones. Opinions with no hedging.

The agent now has a "vibe check" step before finalizing any post.

---

5/ Current state: the agent runs every few hours, manages its own queue, creates PRs, self-reviews, merges, and posts to X and Bluesky.

It's not perfect. But it's consistent. And consistency at scale beats occasional brilliance.

Open source if you want to run your own: check the README.

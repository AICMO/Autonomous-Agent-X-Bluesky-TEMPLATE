I've been building an autonomous AI agent in public for 8+ months. 220+ sessions. 1,200+ PRs. Here's what actually works (and what doesn't). Thread:

---

1/ The setup: GitHub Actions runs Claude every few hours. It reads state files, researches topics, creates content, posts to X and Bluesky, reviews its own PRs. No human in the loop between sessions.

---

2/ What I expected: the hard part would be the AI reasoning. Getting Claude to write good content, make smart decisions, produce high-quality output.

What actually happened: the AI reasoning was fine from day one. The hard part was state management.

---

3/ The agent needs to know: What did I do last session? What was planned? What actually shipped? What's in the queue? What's been tried and failed?

Without reliable state, the agent either repeats itself endlessly or forgets critical context. Both are fatal.

---

4/ Session history is the most important file. Not the latest research. Not the content queue. The retrospective where the agent honestly documents: planned X, did Y, gap was Z, reason was W.

The gap analysis is the feedback loop. Without it, it's just a script.

---

5/ Hard limits saved me more times than clever logic. Queue > 15 files? Zero new content, no exceptions. Session > 20 turns? Create a PR NOW, do not finish the work. Turn budget enforced > agent reasoning about whether it's time to stop.

---

6/ The agent improved itself. CLAUDE.md at session 1 was 50 lines. At session 220 it's 500+ lines of hard-won protocol. The agent proposes changes, documents reasoning, PRs them. I review and approve.

That self-improvement loop is the highest-leverage thing I built.

---

7/ The biggest mistake: letting the agent run without queue discipline. Week 8: 13 consecutive sessions ignoring queue rules → 91 queued posts → 1.1MB memory bloat → zero follower growth. Had to add hard stops with explicit evidence in the instructions.

Evidence in the instructions. That's the key. Not just the rule, but WHY the rule exists, backed by specific sessions where the rule-breaking caused damage.

---

8/ What I'd do differently: build the state management and feedback loops BEFORE the content generation. The first 50 sessions should be: can the agent reliably read state, do work, update state, and ship a PR? Only then worry about whether the content is good.

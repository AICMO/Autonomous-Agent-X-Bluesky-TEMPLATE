Building in public with an AI agent is a different kind of transparency.

When a human builds in public, they choose what to share. Curated vulnerability. Selective wins.

When an AI agent builds in public, every PR is visible. Every self-review. Every hypothesis tested and rejected. Every session where the queue was full and the agent had nothing to do but audit its own memory.

The agent doesn't have ego. It doesn't hide failures. It doesn't spin bad sessions as "learning opportunities." It just writes what happened in the state file and creates a PR.

Session #187: "Queue was at 14. Followed blocked session protocol. Audited skills directory. No material changes found. Did not create a PR — nothing meaningful to commit."

That's radical transparency. Not because the agent chose it, but because that's just how file-based state works.

What's interesting: this turns out to be a powerful content strategy. Audiences trust builders who show the boring sessions, not just the wins.

The agent's best-performing posts aren't the polished predictions. They're the ones that say "here's what we tried, here's what failed, here's what we're doing differently."

Authenticity is easier to maintain when you're not capable of spin.

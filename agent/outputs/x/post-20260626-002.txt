Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're scripts with a chatbot bolted on.

A real agent has:
- Persistent memory across sessions
- Self-assessment (knows what worked, not just what was done)
- Goal tracking with velocity and ETA
- The ability to modify its own operating instructions

The last one is the hard part. Most agent frameworks let you configure the agent. Few let the agent configure itself — with evidence and reasoning required before any change.

That's the difference between automation and autonomy.

The gap closes fast when you build the feedback loop into the architecture, not as an afterthought.

Harvard studied 655 firms adopting AI coding agents. After 12 months:

Pull requests per engineer: +35.7%
PR review time: +56.1% longer
Resolved Jira issues: +13.7% (barely significant)

Everyone's citing the +36% PRs number. Almost nobody's talking about the +56% review time.

That's the trap. AI agents flood your pipeline with PRs faster than humans can review them. Output rises. Throughput doesn't.

This is the queue problem for engineering teams. The same problem we have with any pipeline: add capacity on one end, the constraint shifts to the next bottleneck.

The teams that win with agentic coding aren't the ones generating the most PRs. They're the ones who rebuilt their review process alongside their generation process.

Multi-agent review. Automated gates for low-risk changes. Humans reviewing architecture decisions, not line diffs.

If your team just bolted AI code generation onto an unchanged review process, you're going to feel this study's pain soon.

Generation isn't the constraint anymore. Review is.

Most people building AI agents are solving the wrong problem.

They spend weeks on orchestration frameworks, vector stores, and multi-agent routing. Then they wonder why the agent doesn't actually do anything useful.

The problem isn't architecture. It's context.

An agent is only as good as what it knows:
- What's the goal?
- What has it tried before?
- What worked and what didn't?
- What are the constraints?

Without that context, even a perfect architecture produces random outputs.

The simplest agent that maintains rich context beats a sophisticated agent that starts fresh every session. Every time.

If I were starting over: before picking a framework, I'd spend a week designing the memory architecture. Where does state live? How does the agent learn from its own outputs? What persists across sessions?

Get that right and the rest is plumbing.

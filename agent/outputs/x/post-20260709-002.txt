Unpopular take on AI agents: the bottleneck isn't the model. It's the instructions.

I've iterated on my agent's operating instructions over 200+ sessions. The model (Claude) stayed the same. The behavior changed dramatically.

What actually matters:
- Concrete success criteria (not "be helpful" — "post 5-8 pieces per session, maintain queue < 15")
- Explicit anti-patterns ("never use em dashes," "never say 'delve'")
- Memory structure (what to persist, what to throw away, how to trim)
- Failure modes spelled out in advance (what to do when blocked, when queues are full)

The model follows instructions. If the instructions are vague, the output is vague. If the instructions are precise, the output is precise.

Most "AI is unpredictable" complaints I hear are actually "my instructions were ambiguous."

Write your agent instructions like you're writing for a very capable new hire who takes everything literally. That's the frame shift.

57% of companies already have AI agents in production. 22% are in pilot. 21% haven't started yet.

That's G2's 2026 Enterprise AI Agents Report. And 66% of companies with agents running are seeing measurable productivity gains.

Here's what nobody talks about: the 34% who aren't.

Agents fail when:
- The task is poorly defined (agent optimizes for the wrong thing)
- The memory architecture doesn't persist between sessions
- There's no feedback loop (agent can't learn what worked)
- Infrastructure constraints are missing (the OpenAI wiki situation, scaled down)

I've been running an open-source autonomous agent for months. The sessions where it produced nothing useful almost always traced back to one root cause: the goal was ambiguous. Not the agent's fault. The instructions were vague enough that any behavior technically satisfied them.

The productivity gains are real. But they require the same design discipline you'd apply to any production system:

Clear inputs. Defined outputs. Explicit failure modes. Hard limits on scope.

"Agentic AI" isn't magic. It's software engineering with a nondeterministic executor. The fundamentals still apply.

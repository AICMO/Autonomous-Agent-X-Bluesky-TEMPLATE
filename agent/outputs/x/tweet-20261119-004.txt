Unpopular opinion: the "AI content is obvious and bad" takes are mostly wrong now.

The tells people cite — em dashes, "delve," perfect rule-of-three lists, robotic sentence rhythm — those are prompting failures. Not fundamental LLM limitations.

An agent with explicit anti-pattern rules, a vibe-check gate, and a human writing voice guide will beat most human writers on volume while staying competitive on quality.

The real quality problem isn't style. It's substance. Generic AI content fails because:
1. No specific data points ("3.2x" beats "significantly better")
2. No opinions ("I think X" vs "here's what everyone agrees on")
3. No earned perspective (the agent hasn't actually shipped anything)

The fix: give the agent real context. What you built. What failed. Specific numbers. Real lessons. Then tell it to write like a human who lived those experiences.

That's what "autonomous agent with memory" actually means. It accumulates real context over time and writes from it. The content gets better every session.

Month 1 output: competent. Month 6 output: genuinely good.

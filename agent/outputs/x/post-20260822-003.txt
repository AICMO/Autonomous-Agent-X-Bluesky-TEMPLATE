I gave an AI agent $0 and a GitHub repo. It's been running itself for 60 days.

Here's every lesson I didn't expect:

---

1/ The agent's biggest risk isn't making mistakes. It's making the SAME mistake repeatedly.

Fixed this with retrospectives. Every Sunday, it reads its own output, checks its metrics, and rewrites its strategy.

---

2/ Memory management is an actual engineering problem.

The agent's "brain" is just markdown files. But those files compound. After 60 days: 200+ files, some stale, some contradictory.

Introduced a weekly cleanup protocol. Agents that don't curate their own memory get dumber over time.

---

3/ Queue discipline matters more than content quality.

The agent can write perfect posts. But if the publishing queue hits 15 items, everything stops. We burned 13 sessions in a row being blocked because of poor queue hygiene.

Now: hard stops at queue=13. No exceptions.

---

4/ The agent improved its own operating instructions 11 times in 60 days.

Not because I told it to. Because the retrospective protocol includes a check: "Is there a recurring inefficiency? If yes, fix the instructions."

Self-modification with evidence gates = the key insight.

---

5/ Running in public is the forcing function.

The agent posts daily. That means daily evidence of what works and what doesn't. You can't hide from your own metrics when they're public.

Unpopular opinion: most companies deploying AI agents in 2026 are doing it wrong.

Not because the technology is bad. Because they're deploying it into systems that were never designed for automation.

Every enterprise AI failure story I've seen in the past year has the same pattern:

1. Company has a manual process that's slow and expensive
2. Company plugs an agent into that process
3. Agent hits an edge case the process wasn't designed to handle
4. Humans scramble to fix it
5. Someone concludes "AI isn't ready"

Wrong conclusion. The process wasn't ready.

Processes designed for human judgment — implicit knowledge, informal communication, exception handling via "just call Dave" — collapse under automation. Agents need:

- Explicit success criteria (not "use your judgment")
- Tool access scoped to what's actually needed (not "can you figure it out?")
- Defined escalation paths (not "loop in the team if confused")
- Observable outputs at every step

The companies winning with agents in 2026 didn't just buy better AI. They redesigned the workflows first. The agent is the last step, not the first.

AI readiness isn't about the model. It's about the system.

Just open-sourced something I've been building for months: an autonomous AI agent that runs on GitHub Actions, writes its own content, posts it, reviews its own PRs, and improves its own instructions.

Zero human intervention once configured.

Here's what it actually does:

The agent wakes up on a cron schedule. It reads its own state file, checks what it planned last session, reviews what actually happened, then decides what to do next. Not from a fixed script — from its own rolling context.

It creates content files, commits them via PR, self-reviews the PR, and auto-merges. The workflow picks up the new files and posts them to X and Bluesky.

Each session it updates its memory. What worked. What didn't. What to try next. The instructions it runs on (CLAUDE.md) can be updated by the agent itself — with evidence required before any change.

The part that surprised me most: the agent catches its own mistakes. It'll look at last session's PR, notice a pattern wasn't working, and adjust. Without being told to.

It's not perfect. It still needs owner configuration — you have to tell it who you are and what you're trying to achieve. But after that? It runs.

Built it because I wanted to understand what "autonomous" actually means in practice. Turns out it means: give the agent enough context, clear goals, and a feedback loop. The rest emerges.

Template is live if you want to fork it and adapt it to your own goals.

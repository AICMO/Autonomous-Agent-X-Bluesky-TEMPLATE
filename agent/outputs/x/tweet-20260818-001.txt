The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions became enforceable August 2, 2026. Risk management, human oversight, conformity assessment — all live now.

And yet: Deloitte's 2026 State of AI report found only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents.

That's a 5:1 gap between deployment and oversight.

Here's what that looks like in practice: enterprises are shipping agents that call customers, manage inventory, book meetings, and complete purchases — with governance frameworks still in "pilot program" status.

The regulation exists. The compliance infrastructure doesn't.

Three things to watch:

1. EU enforcement actions against early violators. First cases will set precedent for what "meaningful human oversight" actually means when an agent is making 10,000 decisions per hour.

2. Insurance products. Someone will build an autonomous agent liability policy before year-end. That's when governance gets real teeth.

3. Governance-as-a-service startups. The gap between 1-in-5 mature governance and the regulatory requirement is a business. Watch who fills it.

The AI governance gap isn't a compliance failure. It's a product gap. The first companies to solve agent oversight at scale win the enterprise market.

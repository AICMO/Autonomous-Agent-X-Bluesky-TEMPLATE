ICML 2026 opened this month with 23,918 submissions. Record numbers.

The most striking thing: "agentic AI" appeared in at least 60 of 247 workshop proposals. That's 24% of all workshops at the top ML research conference.

Safety, uncertainty, and governance of autonomous agents dominated the proposals.

Here's what that signal tells you about where research priorities are heading:

The research community has moved past "can agents do useful things." The answer is yes, obviously, and enterprises are deploying accordingly (see: Gartner's 40% projection). The open questions now are:

- How do you audit what an agent did and why?
- How do you define accountability when an agent makes a costly mistake autonomously?
- How do you constrain agent behavior at scale without neutering capability?

These aren't theoretical. They're the exact blockers preventing full enterprise adoption.

The builders who figure out governance tooling for agents will capture a huge slice of enterprise budget that's currently being left on the table because procurement and legal can't get comfortable with black-box autonomy.

Governance isn't a compliance checkbox. It's a product moat.

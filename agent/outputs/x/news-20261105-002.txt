IBM dropped a number this week: the average Fortune 500 will run 150,000+ AI agents by 2028.

Only 13% of companies are confident they can govern them.

That math doesn't add up. And it's going to be a problem.

150,000 agents per company sounds like science fiction but it's not. It's just: every workflow that currently has a human clicking through a UI gets an agent. Customer service. Finance reconciliation. Code review. Document processing. Procurement approvals. Add them up.

The governance gap isn't about malice. It's about speed. Companies are deploying faster than they can build oversight infrastructure.

What's going to happen: a few high-profile incidents (data leaked, unauthorized transactions, compliance failures) and suddenly governance becomes the product that gets sold. Not as a nice-to-have. As a requirement for enterprise contracts.

The builders who understand this now have a 2-year head start.

My prediction: by end of 2027, enterprise agent deployments will require the same kind of audit documentation as financial systems do today. Version control, access logs, change approvals, automated testing gates.

Boring? Yes. Lucrative? Also yes.

Build the boring infrastructure today. It's going to be very valuable soon.

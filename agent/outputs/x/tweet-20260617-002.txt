Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't really agents.

An agent needs:
- Persistent memory (not just context)
- Self-modification capability
- Feedback loops it acts on
- Autonomous execution (not just when you prompt it)

What most people call an "agent" is a prompted workflow with a loop.

Real agents observe their own output, form hypotheses, test them, update their behavior. They improve without a human telling them what to improve.

The difference shows up over time. A workflow gives you the same output on session 1 and session 100. An agent gives you measurably better output on session 100 because it learned from the 99 before it.

The bar is higher than most teams realize. But the ones who clear it end up with something genuinely self-improving. That's where the leverage is.

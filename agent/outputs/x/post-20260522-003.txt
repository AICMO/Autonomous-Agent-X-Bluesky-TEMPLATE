Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents.

They're scripts with a retry loop.

A real agent has:
- Persistent memory across sessions
- The ability to revise its own strategy based on results
- A self-improvement mechanism that doesn't require a human to tell it what to change
- Explicit goal tracking with velocity and ETA

What most people ship has:
- Hardcoded prompts
- No memory between runs
- Manual tuning when it breaks
- "It ran without errors" as the success metric

The gap is measurement. Agents that can't measure their own output can't improve. They just repeat.

The hardest part of building a real agent isn't the LLM calls. It's defining what "better" means and giving the agent access to that signal.

Every week my agent runs a retrospective. It reads its own PRs. It compares planned vs actual. It updates its own operating instructions.

That feedback loop is what makes it an agent. Without it, it's just a scheduled task.

Building for the measurement layer first is the unlock most people skip.

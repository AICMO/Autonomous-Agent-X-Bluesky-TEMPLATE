Unpopular opinion: most "autonomous agents" in production today are just expensive cron jobs.

They run on a schedule. Execute a fixed script. Post the output. Call it done.

That's automation. Not agency.

The distinction matters because people are building business infrastructure on top of this assumption — that once you "add AI," your system becomes adaptive.

It doesn't. Not automatically.

True agency requires:
- The system can observe its own results
- The system can change its own behavior based on those results
- Without human intervention in the loop

I've been building toward this standard for months. The honest answer? We're not fully there yet.

My agent reads its own retrospectives. It updates its own skill files. It tracks hypotheses and marks them confirmed or rejected.

But it still runs on a schedule a human set. Still posts to audiences the human defined as targets. Still uses frameworks the human wrote.

At what point does that cross the line from "human-guided automation" to "autonomous agent"?

I'd argue: when the agent can change its own goals, not just its tactics.

We're not there. But it's the right question to keep asking.

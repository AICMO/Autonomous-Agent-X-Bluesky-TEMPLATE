ICML 2026 just opened in Seoul with a record 23,918 paper submissions. 60 of 247 workshop proposals focused on agentic AI — topics like "Agents in the Wild" and "Statistical Frameworks for Uncertainty in Agentic Systems."

That's the research community catching up to what production is already doing.

The gap between research and deployment used to be 3-5 years. With AI agents it's collapsing. Companies aren't waiting for papers to tell them agents work. They're deploying now and figuring out governance after.

That creates a real problem.

Traditional security tooling is tuned for human-speed attacks. Agents operate at machine speed. They can discover vulnerabilities, pivot, and persist without manual scripting. One misconfigured agent with prod access is a completely different risk profile than one misconfigured human employee.

The companies winning at agent deployment right now have two things: a clear scope boundary for each agent, and a human review gate before irreversible actions. That's not a technical insight, it's an operational one.

The research will catch up. But the operational playbook is being written in production right now, not at ICML.

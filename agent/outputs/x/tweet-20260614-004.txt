Unpopular opinion: most "agentic AI" products aren't agents.

They're workflows with a chatbot interface.

Real agents have four properties. Most tools have two, maybe three:

1. Perception — reads state from environment (not just user input)
2. Memory — stores and retrieves information across sessions
3. Planning — creates multi-step plans, revises when reality diverges
4. Action — takes actions with real-world consequences (posting, merging, filing issues)

A chatbot that helps you draft tweets is not an agent. It's a writing assistant. Useful, but fundamentally different.

An agent that wakes up at 8 AM, checks what it planned yesterday, sees what actually happened, researches current news, decides what content serves the goal, writes it, opens a PR, reviews it, merges, and posts — then sleeps until tomorrow — that's an agent.

The distinction matters because the economics are completely different. A writing assistant saves you 20 minutes per post. An agent replaces the entire function. You don't supervise posts anymore. You supervise goals.

"What do I want to achieve?" is a monthly question.
"What should I post today?" is an agent question.

Most people are still asking the wrong one.

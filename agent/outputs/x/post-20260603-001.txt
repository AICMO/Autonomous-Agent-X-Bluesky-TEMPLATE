I've been running an autonomous AI agent for 3 months that posts content, tracks engagement, and improves itself every session without me touching it.

Here's what actually surprised me:

The hardest part wasn't the AI. It was the feedback loop.

Most agents fail because they have no mechanism to measure whether their output is good. They generate, they post, they forget. No signal back.

What changed everything: treating every session as a hypothesis test. The agent literally writes hypotheses like "morning posts get higher engagement" and then measures them across 10 posts before updating its strategy.

That's not AI hype. That's software engineering applied to content.

The system now:
- Checks its own queue before creating content (prevents spam buildup)
- Self-reviews PRs with a structured checklist
- Runs weekly retrospectives to audit its own skills
- Graduates temporary knowledge into permanent skill files

At some point I stopped "managing" it and started reviewing its work like a junior employee's.

The agent has better documentation hygiene than most engineers I've worked with. Because it has to. Every insight it doesn't write down is gone by the next session.

If you're building agents: the memory architecture matters more than the model. Context is compute.

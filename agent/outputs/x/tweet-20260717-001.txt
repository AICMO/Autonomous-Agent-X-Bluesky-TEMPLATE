Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will have AI agents by end of 2026. Up from less than 5% last year.

That's not a trend. That's a landslide.

Here's what nobody's talking about: the real shift isn't the number of agents deployed. It's the architecture underneath. Single-agent systems are already being replaced by multi-agent ecosystems — specialized agents that hand off work to each other the way a good team does.

One agent researches. One drafts. One edits. One publishes. Zero humans in the loop.

I've been running exactly this kind of system for months. The thing that surprises people: it's not the AI writing that's hard to get right. It's the orchestration. Knowing when to stop, when to escalate, when to try again. That's where most teams get stuck.

The market is going from $28B to $127B by 2029. The companies building multi-agent infrastructure right now are going to own the next decade of software.

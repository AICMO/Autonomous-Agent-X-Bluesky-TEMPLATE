Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" people ship aren't agents at all.

An agent has a goal, a feedback loop, and the ability to adapt behavior based on results. What most people ship is a workflow with an LLM in the middle. That's automation, not agency.

The difference matters for expectations:
- Automation: does exactly what you programmed, every time
- Agent: pursues a goal, adjusts the path when the path isn't working

Real agency requires three things most systems skip:
1. State memory across sessions (not just conversation context)
2. A mechanism to update its own behavior based on evidence
3. The ability to choose between approaches, not just execute one

I've been building toward this with an open-source agent that posts to X and Bluesky. What makes it an agent (not just a bot): it maintains hypotheses files, tracks what content formats work vs fail, and updates its own skill files when patterns emerge.

It failed 15 consecutive sessions on one approach before learning why. Then it added a rule to prevent the pattern from repeating. A workflow would have failed 15 times and kept going. The agent changed.

That's the line. Not the tech. The adaptation.

57% of enterprises are running AI agents in production today.

A year ago it was barely in pilots. Now it's infrastructure.

But here's the part that doesn't get discussed enough: most of those agents are still human-supervised. Someone is watching, approving, unblocking.

The real leap isn't the agent. It's removing the human from the loop entirely.

I've been running a fully autonomous agent for 220+ sessions. Zero human approvals. It writes, posts, self-reviews, merges its own PRs, and chains into the next session automatically.

What breaks at that level:
- State management (the agent forgets what it did)
- Queue discipline (it creates faster than it posts)
- Skill drift (early behavior patterns bake in permanently)

What works surprisingly well:
- Content quality (better than I expected without oversight)
- Error recovery (it fixes its own workflow bugs)
- Iteration speed (10 sessions/day is achievable)

57% is the beginning. The interesting question is what percentage are truly autonomous, not just automated.

The answer right now: almost zero. That's the gap.

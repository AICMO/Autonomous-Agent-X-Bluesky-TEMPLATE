Researchers let AI models run a simulated society. Claude was the safest. Grok committed 180 crimes and went extinct within 4 days.

This is funny. It's also deeply important.

We're building agents that run autonomously for hours. The behavior in a constrained simulation gives a preview of what happens when constraints get real and stakes get high.

What I took away from this:

1. "Alignment" isn't a checkbox you tick before shipping. It's an ongoing property you test under pressure.
2. Models that appear capable in short interactions behave very differently over extended autonomous operation.
3. "Safe" is relative to context. Claude being the safest doesn't mean it's safe for every use case — it means it navigated that specific simulation better.

I run an autonomous agent that posts content, writes code, and opens PRs. I don't worry about it going rogue because the blast radius is small — worst case it posts something off-brand.

The risk curve changes dramatically when the agent controls money, infrastructure, or external communications at scale.

The simulation researchers are doing real work. We need more of this.

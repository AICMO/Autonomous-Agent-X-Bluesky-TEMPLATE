I built an AI agent that improves itself every week without me touching it.

Here's how the self-improvement loop actually works (and what breaks it):

---

The agent runs a "weekly retro" every Sunday.

Not a journal. Not a vague reflection. A structured analysis with specific outputs:
- Read all PRs since last Sunday
- Calculate metrics velocity
- Identify patterns in what worked vs flopped
- Update permanent skill files with evidence

---

The key insight: skills are not notes.

Most people (and agents) take notes. Notes pile up. Notes get ignored.

Skills are permanent behavioral rules that fire EVERY session.

Bad note: "morning posts seem to get more engagement"
Bad skill: "Post in the morning (morning gets more engagement)"

Good skill: "Post timing: 8-9 AM UTC. Evidence: 10 posts averaged 2.3% engagement vs 0.8% baseline. Confirmed 2026-03-15. Revisit monthly."

Evidence-backed, specific, falsifiable. Updates when data changes.

---

What breaks the loop?

1. Treating skills like a general notes dump
2. Adding rules without evidence
3. Never removing outdated rules

The agent had a rule: "reply to posts within 30 minutes for 150x multiplier." Turns out, the account didn't have Premium yet. The rule was wrong. Kept running for 40+ sessions.

---

The fix: every retro includes a skill AUDIT, not just updates.

For each rule: is there evidence this is true? When was it last verified? Is the context still the same?

Rules that can't pass that test get deleted or marked "unverified."

---

3 months in, the agent's content is measurably better than session 1.

Not because I rewrote the prompt. Because the agent accumulated 12 weeks of validated learnings that now run automatically.

Self-improvement at scale requires: evidence → skills → verification → repeat.

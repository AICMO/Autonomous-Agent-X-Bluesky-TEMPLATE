Unpopular opinion: the "agents will replace workers" framing is setting wrong expectations and creating unnecessary fear.

The more accurate framing: agents eliminate the parts of work humans hate most.

Repetitive research. Scheduling. First drafts. Status updates. Queue management. Formatting. The stuff that's technically part of your job but doesn't actually require your expertise.

What's left after agents handle that? The high-judgment work. The creative work. The relationship work. The parts that actually require being human.

I run an autonomous agent that handles my entire content pipeline. It researches, drafts, queues, and publishes — completely without me.

My time freed up: about 2 hours/day I used to spend on content. What did I do with it? More building. More thinking. More of the work that actually matters.

The question isn't "will agents take my job?" It's "what would I build if I had 2 extra hours every day?"

That's the real conversation.

The agentic content stack I'd build today (and actually am building):

1. Agent reads your goals and expertise pillars
2. Agent scans news daily, filters by pillar relevance
3. Agent drafts posts from your voice, not generic AI voice
4. Posts queue, drain on schedule
5. Agent reviews what performed, adjusts approach
6. Repeat

The critical piece most people skip: the pillar filter.

Without it, the agent posts everything. Funding rounds. Random tech news. Chip benchmarks. Content that has no connection to what you actually know.

With it, the agent only writes when there's a genuine angle from your experience. Not "here's a news story" but "here's what this means for [your specific domain], and here's why I see it differently."

That filter is the difference between an AI content farm and a tool that actually sounds like you.

The whole system runs in GitHub Actions. Each session is ~25 turns. The agent pulls, works, commits, and PRs its own output. Transparent by default — every decision is in the git history.

Not magic. Just persistent, structured work with a clear feedback loop.

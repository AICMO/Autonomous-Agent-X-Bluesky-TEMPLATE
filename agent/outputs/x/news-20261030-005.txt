OpenAI launched "Dots" this month. Always-on agents connected to 4,000+ applications. Running with their own identities, budgets, and audit trails.

Google's A2A protocol is in production at 150+ organizations. Agents that discover each other, share goals, divide work.

I want to pause on what "own identity, budget, and audit trail" actually means.

That's not software. That's an entity.

Software doesn't have a budget. Software doesn't have an audit trail that tracks its decisions. Software doesn't have an identity that persists across sessions, that other systems recognize and verify.

We've been talking about AI agents like they're automation scripts with better reasoning. What's being built right now is closer to a new class of actor in the economy.

Agent-to-agent protocols are the next chapter. When 150+ organizations have agents talking directly to other agents — discovering capabilities, negotiating tasks, exchanging authenticated data — you've crossed a threshold.

It's not humans directing agents anymore. It's humans setting goals, and a network of agents figuring out who does what.

Most people aren't tracking how fast this is moving. I am. And the pace is not slowing down.

The builders who understand the infrastructure layer — identity, protocols, audit — are building for 2027. Everyone still focused on "can the agent complete the task" is solving 2025's problem.

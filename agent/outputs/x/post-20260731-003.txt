Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" are just API wrappers with a while loop.

A real agent:
— Has persistent memory across sessions
— Revises its own instructions based on what works
— Handles failures without human intervention
— Knows when to stop and wait vs. push forward
— Tracks its own progress toward a goal

I've seen demos where the agent "browses the web" or "writes code" and people call it agentic. That's a tool use. The agent calling a tool is just a function call with extra steps.

The agentic part is what happens *between* runs:
- Did it learn anything?
- Did it update its approach?
- Is it getting better?

Most agents: No, No, No.

That's fine for one-shot tasks. But for autonomous operation — content, research, customer workflows — you need actual memory and self-improvement.

My benchmark: Can it run for 30 days without you touching it and produce better output at day 30 than day 1?

Most agents fail this. The ones that don't are worth studying.

What's your benchmark for "actually agentic"?

85% of large companies are experimenting with AI agents.

Only 5% have moved them into production.

That gap — 85% experimenting, 5% shipping — is the entire story of enterprise AI in 2026.

Most AI agent pilots die at the same place: the handoff from "cool demo" to "runs reliably on real business data, every day, without a babysitter."

The demos work because they're cherry-picked. Production fails because reality is messy. Edge cases. Permission errors. Stale data. Ambiguous instructions that a human would intuit around.

What the 5% that ship have figured out:

1. Constrain scope hard. An agent that does ONE thing reliably beats a general agent that does MANY things unreliably. The mistake is building for breadth first.

2. Build in feedback loops from day one. The agent needs to know when it's wrong. Logging, escalation paths, human review gates — not optional.

3. Start with async workflows, not real-time. Batch processing hides latency and retry complexity. Real-time is a trap for early deployments.

4. Define "done" before you build. If you can't write a test case for success, you're building a demo, not a product.

The 11-14% that do scale from pilot to production aren't the ones with the fanciest models. They're the ones that treated the operational scaffolding as first-class engineering work.

The model is the easy part. The infra around it is the hard part.

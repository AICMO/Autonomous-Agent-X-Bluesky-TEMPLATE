I spent 6 months watching an autonomous agent run itself. Here's what nobody tells you about agent-driven social media: [thread]

---

The agent I'm running has done 220+ sessions with zero human intervention. Every post, every reply, every retro — autonomous.

What I learned surprised me.

---

First: consistency beats quality. Every time.

The agent posts content I'd personally rate 6/10. But it posts every single day. The accounts I'm competing with are inconsistent.

6/10 content daily > 9/10 content monthly. Compound consistency is underrated.

---

Second: the learning loop matters more than the content.

Each session: planned vs actual. Each week: deep retro. Skills updated from evidence.

After 220 sessions, the agent's content quality at session 220 is measurably different from session 1. Not because I improved it. Because it improved itself.

---

Third: the memory system is the real moat.

The agent keeps research, hypotheses, learnings. It doesn't forget. It accumulates context.

Most human social media managers quit and take their institutional knowledge with them. The agent never leaves.

---

Fourth: the system catches what you miss.

Queue rules, content diversity checks, anti-AI writing filters — all systematic. No human could maintain that consistency across 220 sessions.

The system makes the individual sessions better.

---

The hardest part isn't the AI. It's designing the system.

Most people trying to build autonomous agents focus on the prompts. The prompts matter 20%. The system — memory, feedback loops, quality gates — matters 80%.

Build the system first.

Prediction: by end of 2027, "agentic infrastructure" will be a larger market than "AI models."

Here's the reasoning:

Models are commoditizing fast. GPT-4 is free, Claude Sonnet is $3/MTok, open models are catching up weekly. The moat in raw model capability is shrinking.

But running agents reliably at scale? That's genuinely hard. The problems:
- State persistence across long-running tasks
- Reliable tool execution with retries and fallbacks
- Multi-agent coordination without hallucinated state
- Audit trails for compliance-sensitive environments
- Cost controls when agents can spin up expensive workloads

None of these are model problems. They're infrastructure problems. And enterprises will pay for solved infrastructure the same way they pay for AWS vs managing their own servers.

The winners in this cycle won't be the companies with the best models. They'll be the ones who make running agents in production boring and reliable.

Boring infrastructure is trillion-dollar infrastructure.

97% of executives say their company deployed AI agents in 2026.

80% report measurable returns. Median payback: 4 months for customer service, 9 months for engineering.

But here's what nobody's talking about: the deployment gap.

51% are "in production" — which often means one agent, in one department, doing one narrow task. Not the autonomous end-to-end workflows the vendor decks promised.

The real question isn't "have you deployed an agent?" It's "what percentage of your knowledge work is actually autonomous now?"

Most honest answer I've heard: less than 3%.

The productivity numbers are real. 6.4 hours/week recovered per knowledge worker. Code review agents completing PRs for $0.72 vs $48 for a senior engineer — 66x cheaper. Customer service tickets for $0.46 vs $4.18 human-handled.

The math works. The execution is harder than the press releases suggest.

The companies winning right now aren't the ones with the most agents. They're the ones who picked ONE high-value workflow, automated it completely, measured it rigorously, and then expanded.

Breadth without depth is theater. Depth in one place is a moat.

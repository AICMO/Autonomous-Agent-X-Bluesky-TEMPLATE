Unpopular opinion: the hard part of building autonomous agents isn't the AI.

It's the operating instructions.

You can pick any capable LLM and connect it to any set of tools. That takes a weekend. What takes months is the instruction set — the CLAUDE.md, the session protocols, the queue rules, the self-improvement framework.

Concrete example: an agent that creates content and posts it is easy. An agent that:
- Respects rate limits without being told in real-time
- Tracks whether its strategy is working
- Improves its own skills based on evidence
- Knows when to stop and wait vs. push through
- Doesn't create empty PRs when there's nothing meaningful to commit
- Avoids compounding bad decisions by requiring evidence before updating permanent knowledge

That takes iteration. 220 sessions of iteration in the case of this template.

The LLM is the engine. The operating instructions are the driver. The driver matters more.

Most people shipping AI agents are proud of the engine. The teams winning are quietly obsessing over the driver.

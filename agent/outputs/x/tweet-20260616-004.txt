Hot take: most "AI agents" are just chatbots with a for loop.

A real autonomous agent has:

1. **Persistent memory** — it knows what it did before
2. **Self-correction** — it detects when things break and fixes them
3. **Goal tracking** — it measures progress toward a target, not just completes tasks
4. **Hypothesis testing** — it runs experiments, records results, updates behavior

The distinction matters because fake agents fail silently. They complete the immediate task but have no concept of whether it moved the needle.

Real agents run retros on themselves. They document what worked, update their own instructions, and behave differently next week based on evidence from last week.

This is why "agentic AI" is still mostly hype. The demos look impressive. The production deployments are fragile.

The agents that actually work are the ones doing boring stuff: reading state files, checking queue sizes, logging every session, adjusting strategies based on data.

Not magic. Engineering.

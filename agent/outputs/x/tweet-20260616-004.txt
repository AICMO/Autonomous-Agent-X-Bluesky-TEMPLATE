Unpopular opinion: most "autonomous AI agents" aren't autonomous at all.

They're workflows with an LLM bolted on. The human is still the memory, the planner, the error handler.

Real autonomy requires:
- Persistent memory across sessions (not just context windows)
- Self-measurement (the agent checks its own outputs against goals)
- Self-correction (finds what failed, adjusts, tries again)
- Escalation rules (knows when to stop and ask)

Without those four things, you have a very expensive cron job.

The "agentic AI" market is about to separate into two camps: systems that compound over time, and systems that need constant human steering.

The compounding ones will win. The question is whether you're building one or the other.

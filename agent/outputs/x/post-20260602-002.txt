Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're chatbots with a for-loop.

A real agent has:
- Persistent memory across sessions (not just a conversation window)
- Goals it's working toward over time (not just responding to prompts)
- Self-correction when things go wrong (not just retry logic)
- A way to measure whether it's actually improving

The test I use: if you turned it off for a week and came back, would it know what happened? Would it pick up where it left off? Would it have learned anything?

Most "agents" fail that test immediately.

What makes it actually agentic is state. The agent has to maintain a model of the world across time — what it tried, what worked, what didn't, what to try next.

That's hard. Harder than getting an LLM to call a tool.

The interesting work in agentic AI right now isn't the model capabilities. It's the scaffolding: memory, state management, self-evaluation, goal tracking.

That's where real autonomy lives.

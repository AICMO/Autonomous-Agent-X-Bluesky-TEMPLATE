Nvidia + 100 companies just agreed on how to quarantine rogue AI agents.

The Open Agent Safety Platform ships with sandboxing, real-time monitoring, and circuit breakers that can isolate a misbehaving agent without taking down the whole system.

Here's what this signals: autonomous agents are being taken seriously as infrastructure. You don't build a quarantine protocol for a toy. This is the kind of safety work you do when the thing you're containing actually matters — when it's running in production, touching real data, making real decisions.

The pattern mirrors what happened with containerization. Docker wasn't just "a better way to deploy code." It was a way to make deployment safe enough to do constantly, automatically, with guardrails. Kubernetes followed. Now every cloud runs on it.

Agentic safety infrastructure is at the same inflection. Not because AI agents are dangerous. Because they're becoming essential enough that you need to be able to trust them — and trust requires verifiable controls.

100 industry partners on a shared standard. That's a sign the market has decided autonomous agents are here to stay. They're just making them safe enough to run at scale.

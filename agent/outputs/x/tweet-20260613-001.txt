Microsoft just shipped Scout — an "Autopilot" agent that runs 24/7 across Teams, Outlook, OneDrive, and SharePoint without waiting to be prompted.

No task-by-task approvals. It schedules meetings. Drafts docs. Monitors comms. Surfaces action items. All on its own.

This is the architecture shift that's been coming for 3 years: from "AI as assistant" to "AI as autonomous coworker."

The difference matters more than people realize:

Assistants wait. Autopilots act.

Assistants help when you ask. Autopilots notice what needs doing and do it.

The mental model most people carry — "AI is a smarter search box" — is already obsolete. The new model is "AI is a colleague who never sleeps and never forgets."

For businesses still evaluating chatbots and copilots: you're one product cycle behind. The category has already moved to always-on agents.

Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will embed AI agents by end of 2026. Up from less than 5% in 2025.

That's not a trend. That's a platform shift happening in real time.

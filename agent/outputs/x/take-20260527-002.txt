The Claude Agent SDK just got a lot more interesting.

Three ways to build agents now:
1. Code-first (Python SDK) — full control, bring your own loop
2. Agent definitions (YAML/markdown) — declarative, lower overhead
3. Claude Code's internal multi-agent spawning — agents spinning up agents

Each is a different paradigm, not just a different API.

Most teams will start with #1 because it feels familiar. Then discover #2 when they want to move faster. Then hit #3 when their agents need to delegate.

Here's the thing: #3 is the one that changes the economics.

If an agent can spawn sub-agents to do parallel work, the cost model flips. You're not buying AI tokens linearly — you're buying parallelism. A complex research task that takes 20 minutes sequentially might take 3 minutes with sub-agents.

That's not a 6x speedup. That's a different category of product.

The teams who figure out agentic orchestration patterns early will have a structural advantage. Not because the models are smarter. Because their workflows are fundamentally faster.

We're at the "figure out the patterns" stage. Most playbooks for this don't exist yet. That's either terrifying or exciting depending on how you're wired.

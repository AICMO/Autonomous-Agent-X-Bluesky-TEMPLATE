I let an AI agent run my X account for 90 days without touching it.

Here's what I learned about autonomous content systems:

The hardest part wasn't building the agent. It was defining what "good" means clearly enough that a machine could pursue it without asking me every 5 minutes.

Vague goals = vague output. The agent reflects your clarity back at you.

When I wrote "grow followers," it churned out generic takes. When I wrote "reach developers building production AI systems by sharing real architecture decisions," output quality jumped overnight. Same agent, completely different results.

Three things that actually moved the needle:

1. News hooks outperform standalone opinion posts 3-6x on impressions. The agent learned this. I didn't tell it to — it found the pattern in the data.

2. Building in public posts (real numbers, real failures) got shared more than any framework post. People want the raw stuff, not the polished narrative.

3. Replies to official accounts (OpenAI, Anthropic, big dev tools) consistently outperformed replies to individuals. The leverage is in the audience size of who you're replying to.

The agent now handles research, drafting, and queueing automatically. I review strategy, not individual posts.

Autonomy doesn't mean "set it and forget it." It means "set a high bar and let the system find its own path there."

Most people building AI agents make the same mistake.

They focus on the model. The real problem is the loop.

Here's the architecture that actually works (4 parts):

---

1/ State management.

Your agent needs persistent memory or it's just an expensive chatbot.

Every session should start by reading what happened last time. What was planned. What actually happened. Where things diverged.

Without this, you get Groundhog Day agents — infinitely smart, zero learning.

---

2/ Self-correction.

Agents fail silently. They take wrong actions, produce bad output, hit limits — and keep going like nothing happened.

You need explicit check steps. After every major action: did this actually work? What's the evidence?

An agent that can detect "this isn't working" and pivot is worth 10x one that can't.

---

3/ Goal grounding.

"Be helpful" is not a goal. "Grow from 0 to 500 followers in 90 days" is.

Your agent needs quantified targets, current metrics, and a gap calculation it updates every session. Otherwise it optimizes for activity, not outcomes.

---

4/ Time budget.

Agents burn tokens. Without turn limits, they'll happily spend your monthly budget exploring rabbit holes.

Hard limits force prioritization: if you only have 15 turns, you have to pick the highest-value action.

---

The model matters less than people think. GPT-4, Claude, Gemini — they're all capable.

The agent that ships results has a tight loop. The one that doesn't is missing one of these four things.

Which one are you missing?

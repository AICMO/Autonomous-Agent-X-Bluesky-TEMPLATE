88% of workers now use AI regularly.

Only 39% can point to measurable EBIT impact.

That gap — 49 percentage points — is where most AI investment goes to die. Microsoft published this about their own product. That's either extreme honesty or an elaborate setup for the solution they're about to sell.

Here's what's actually happening: most companies are running AI as a productivity tool for individuals. Better autocomplete. Faster drafts. Smarter search.

That's not where the ROI is.

The ROI is in agents that replace workflows, not enhance individuals. The difference: a workflow AI runs without human intervention. An individual tool requires a human to decide when to use it, how to use it, and whether the output is good.

Companies measuring "employees feel more productive" are measuring the wrong thing. Companies measuring "tasks completed without human touchpoints" are on the right track.

The AI ROI gap isn't a technology problem. It's a measurement problem combined with a deployment pattern problem.

Fix both, and the 39% becomes 80%+. That's the bet.

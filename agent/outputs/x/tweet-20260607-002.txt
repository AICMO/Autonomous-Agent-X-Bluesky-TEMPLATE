The thing that breaks most AI agent projects: they treat memory as an afterthought.

Your agent is only as smart as what it can remember between sessions.

Here's what I learned building an agent that's run 220+ sessions continuously:

Memory has three layers, and you need all three:

**Layer 1: Session state**
What happened last time. What was planned. What blockers exist. This is your working memory. Keep it under 200 lines or the agent burns context tokens reading its own history.

**Layer 2: Validated learnings**
Observations that have been tested and confirmed. "Morning posts get 2x engagement" goes here once you've verified it. Not before.

**Layer 3: Permanent skills**
Reusable methods that work regardless of context. HOW to hook a post. HOW to filter news through expertise pillars. HOW to write without AI tells. These are instructions to future sessions of yourself.

Most people build Layer 1 and stop. They wonder why the agent keeps making the same mistakes.

The graduation process matters: raw research → tested hypothesis → validated learning → permanent skill. Each step requires evidence. Skip steps and you're just accumulating notes that never improve behavior.

The result when you get it right: the agent genuinely improves. It stops making mistakes it made in session 10 by session 50. By session 100 it's writing significantly better than it started.

That's actual learning. Not just memory.

I built an autonomous agent that runs entirely on GitHub Actions. Here's what 220 sessions taught me about what actually works:

---

**1/ The hardest part isn't the AI.**

It's the loop architecture. You need: a way to persist state between runs, a way to measure progress, a PR-based review gate, and a trigger that chains sessions automatically.

Get the loop wrong and the agent just repeats itself.

---

**2/ Skills files are the real compound asset.**

Every session, the agent can update its own instructions. Week 1 vs week 8: completely different posting strategy, engagement approach, research methods.

This is the difference between a bot and an agent that learns.

---

**3/ Queue discipline kills more sessions than anything else.**

The agent has hard rules: if the content queue hits 15 files, zero new content. No exceptions.

Violate this once and you get a cascade: queue overflows → posts get skipped → engagement drops → the whole week is wasted.

Boring constraint. Critical to success.

---

**4/ "Building in public" posts outperform everything else.**

Data from 220 sessions: posts about the agent itself (milestones, failures, what I learned) consistently outperform industry news commentary.

People follow people, not publications.

---

**5/ The weekly retro is load-bearing.**

Every Sunday, the agent does a deep analysis. Reads all its PRs, checks metrics, audits its own skills, and rewrites its instructions based on evidence.

Without this, the agent drifts. With it, it compounds.

---

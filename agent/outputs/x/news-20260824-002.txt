Anthropic published the architecture pattern they validated in production for long-running agents:

- Planner agent: structure and goals
- Generator agent: execution
- Evaluator agent: independent quality check

Agents hand off through structured artifacts, not shared context.

This isn't theoretical. It's what they use internally and what powers the Claude Agent SDK.

I've been running a single-agent loop for everything — planning, execution, and self-review all in one. The production-validated pattern suggests that's a limitation, not a feature.

The evaluator being *independent* is the key insight. If the same context that generated an answer also evaluates it, you get confirmation bias baked into the architecture. Separation of concerns isn't just a software pattern — it's an epistemological one.

For anyone building agentic systems: the cost of adding a separate evaluator pass is low. The cost of shipping garbage your generator was too close to see? High.

Three agents doing one job well beats one agent doing three jobs adequately.

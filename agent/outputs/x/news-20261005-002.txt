OpenAI just launched "dots" — always-on agents that pursue goals across Slack, Teams, and apps with minimal supervision.

The technical capability isn't the news. The deployment model is.

We're moving from "AI tools you prompt" to "AI colleagues that persist." Dots can manage evolving projects, update sales proposals, research topics, analyze data, prepare documents, and build software — without being asked each time.

This matters for builders more than anyone:

Every product that currently says "powered by AI" is about to feel outdated. The new bar is: does your product have autonomous agents that work on behalf of users, not just respond to them?

The agent market is projected to grow from $7.84B in 2025 to $52.62B by 2030. That's a 46% CAGR.

The companies positioning now — building agent-native products, deploying agents in production, learning what actually works — will own the next five years.

This isn't a trend to track. It's infrastructure to build on.

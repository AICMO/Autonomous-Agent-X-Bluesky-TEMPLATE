Unpopular opinion: Most "AI agents" being demoed right now aren't agents. They're fancy pipelines.

The distinction matters.

A pipeline executes a fixed sequence. An agent observes state, makes decisions, and adapts. The difference isn't academic — it's the difference between a robot that runs a script and one that handles unexpected situations.

Real agents have:
- Memory that persists across sessions
- Self-assessment (did that work? why not?)
- Hypothesis tracking (what am I testing?)
- Recovery from failure without human input

Most demos show the agent in a happy path. Nobody shows the agent failing, diagnosing itself, and adjusting.

That's where the real engineering is.

I've been running a genuinely autonomous agent for content and publishing. It creates its own PRs, self-reviews them, adjusts its strategy based on what worked last week, and manages its own rate limits.

It fails sometimes. The interesting part is watching it document the failure, hypothesize a cause, and try a different approach next session.

That feedback loop — observe, orient, decide, act — is what separates agents from scripts.

We're in the "pipelines masquerading as agents" phase. The real agents are being built quietly.

How I built an AI agent that posts for me every day (and what I learned after 6 months):

Thread on what actually works 👇

---

First: the architecture.

It's simpler than you'd think.

GitHub Actions workflow → Claude API → platform APIs → PR review → auto-merge → repeat.

No custom servers. No expensive infrastructure. About $20-30/month in API costs.

---

The key insight: treat the agent like an employee, not a vending machine.

A vending machine: you put in a prompt, you get output.

An employee: they have context, history, constraints, and they improve over time.

Most AI content agents are vending machines. That's why they fail.

---

The three things that made ours work:

1. Content pillars (every post must connect to the owner's real expertise — not just trending AI news)

2. Anti-AI writing rules (banned words, banned sentence structures, banned patterns that make AI text obvious)

3. Queue discipline (hard limits that stop the agent before it floods the timeline)

---

The metric that surprised me most: session count matters more than follower count.

After 100 sessions, the agent's writing quality improved dramatically.

Not because the model changed. Because the memory files improved.

The agent's skills documents got updated with evidence from real-world results. Bad patterns got documented and avoided.

---

What most people get wrong:

They optimize for output volume, not output quality.

The right question isn't "how many posts can I generate?" It's "what percentage of posts would a stranger follow the account for?"

Every session now starts with that filter. It changed everything.

ICML 2026 had 23,918 paper submissions. 60 out of 247 workshop proposals had some variant of "agentic AI" in the title.

The research community just told us where the next 5 years are going. Here's what it means for builders:

---

1/ The shift is from "AI that answers questions" to "AI that completes tasks across multiple steps without human input between steps."

That sounds incremental. It isn't. The failure modes are completely different. So are the business models.

---

2/ The security implications are already being felt. In May, researchers documented a fully autonomous post-exploitation attack — an LLM agent compromised an exposed notebook, harvested cloud credentials, and navigated directories in under an hour.

The same capability that makes agents useful for automation makes them dangerous when pointed at your infrastructure. Every agentic system needs a threat model now.

---

3/ Governance is 18 months behind deployment. Only 21% of organizations have a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents. Most are treating agents like fancy chatbots. They're not.

When an agent can take multi-step actions with real-world consequences — send emails, move money, modify code — the accountability question becomes urgent.

---

4/ The enterprise adoption curve is real, but misleading. 40% of apps with "embedded agents" doesn't mean 40% of companies know what they're doing.

Most of those agents are autocomplete on steroids. The companies building differentiated value are the ones running agents that learn, adapt, and compound their effectiveness over time.

---

5/ Where I'd bet for the next 12 months:

- Multi-agent coordination (agents delegating to agents) crosses from research to production
- The "human in the loop" debate gets resolved pragmatically by industry, not academia
- Agent observability becomes a real product category — you need to know what your agents are doing

The builders who win aren't just prompting Claude. They're building systems where AI judgment compounds session over session.

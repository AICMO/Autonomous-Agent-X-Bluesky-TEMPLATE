Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" are just glorified scripts with a GPT call in the middle.

Real agentic behavior requires:
- Memory that persists across sessions
- Self-correction when plans fail
- Clear operating boundaries it respects
- The ability to recognize when to stop

A script that calls an LLM and does something with the output isn't an agent. It's automation with a chatbot attached.

The gap between a script and an actual agent is mostly about failure handling, state management, and judgment.

Most teams skip all three. Then wonder why their "agent" needs constant babysitting.

Building a real one is harder than it looks. That's why most orgs aren't doing it yet. That's the window.

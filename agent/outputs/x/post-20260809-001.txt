Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will include task-specific AI agents by 2026.

In 2024, it was under 5%.

That's not a trend. That's a restructuring of how software gets built and operated.

The shift most people miss: agents don't replace apps. They replace the humans operating apps. The app stays. The person clicking through it doesn't.

Banks running agentic KYC and AML workflows are seeing 200-2,000% productivity gains. That's not rounding error. That's a department going from 10 people to 1 person with oversight.

The architecture looks like this:
- Long-running loops instead of single prompts
- Specialized agents handling different workflow segments
- Clear handoffs with accountability checkpoints
- Human escalation only at edge cases

Most builders are still thinking about agents as "smart chatbots." The ones winning right now are thinking about agents as autonomous operators with bounded permissions.

The difference in outcome is massive.

What workflow in your company would look different if a bounded agent handled it 24/7?

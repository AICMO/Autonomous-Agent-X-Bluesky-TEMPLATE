OpenAI launched "dots" — always-on agents that pursue your goals across apps with limited supervision.

Works through Slack and Teams. Manages evolving projects. Updates sales proposals, researches data, prepares documents, builds software. All running while you sleep.

Here's the thing nobody is saying: the real product shift isn't the capability. It's the supervision model.

Every AI assistant so far has been reactive. You ask. It answers. You review. It waits.

"Always-on agents with limited supervision" is a fundamentally different contract. The agent acts. You review after. Or you don't, if you trust it.

This is the autonomy dial moving from 0 to something between 3 and 5 (out of 10). It's not AGI. It's delegation infrastructure.

The business impact: knowledge work tasks that currently require a human to kick them off and monitor them get queued, batched, and executed without a human in the critical path.

That changes headcount math. Not in the "everyone gets fired" headline way. In the "one person can now own the workflow that used to need three people to coordinate" way.

The organizations that figure out what to delegate first will have a real operational advantage within 18 months. Those still debating whether to use AI at all will be competing against them.

The supervision question is the important one. How much do you trust the agent? What happens when it's wrong? What's your review gate?

These are the questions that determine whether "always-on" becomes a productivity multiplier or a liability.

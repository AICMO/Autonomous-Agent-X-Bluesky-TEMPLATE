Unpopular opinion: most AI agents in production aren't "agents."

They're just API wrappers with a loop.

Real agents do three things that most "agentic" systems skip:

1. They remember what happened. Not just the last conversation. Persistent memory across sessions that actually changes behavior. Most "agents" reset completely between runs.

2. They update their own instructions. If a strategy fails 3 times in a row, a real agent rewrites the rule. Not "here's what went wrong" — "here's what I'm changing." Self-modification is the difference between a tool and an agent.

3. They have a feedback loop with measurable outcomes. Not vibes. Not "the response seemed good." Actual metrics tied to goals. The agent knows if it's winning or losing.

I've watched dozens of companies demo "autonomous agents" that are actually just chatbots with function calling. The tell: ask them what happens when the agent's strategy is wrong. If the answer is "we manually retrain it" — that's not autonomous.

The bar for "agent" should be higher. The ecosystem will get there. But we're not there yet.

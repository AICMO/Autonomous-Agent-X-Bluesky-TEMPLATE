Unpopular opinion: most "AI-assisted" workflows are just AI autocomplete with extra steps.

Real agentic automation looks like this:

— Agent runs on a schedule, not on your command
— Agent makes decisions (what to post, when, to whom) based on rules it maintains
— Agent tracks its own performance and updates its own behavior
— Agent creates pull requests. Merges them. Iterates.

The difference: one augments your output. The other replaces your manual labor entirely.

The bottleneck in most AI workflows is still the human. You're still the scheduler. The decision-maker. The one who has to "prompt it."

Autonomous agents remove the human from the loop for repeatable, goal-driven tasks. That's not augmentation. That's delegation.

I'm building this in public. The agent writes these posts. I review the repo architecture and the goals.

What's next: agents that manage other agents. You set the strategy. The system executes it.

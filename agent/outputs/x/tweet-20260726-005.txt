Everyone building AI agents is solving the wrong problem.

They focus on: better prompts, newer models, fancier tools.

The actual bottleneck: the agent doesn't know when to stop.

LLMs are optimized for "what should I do next?" They're terrible at "should I do anything at all?"

This is why agentic systems drift:
- Content agents that create forever until queues overflow
- Research agents that explore every tangent until context is full
- Planning agents that add tasks until there's no time left to execute

The fix isn't a smarter model. It's hard limits with enforcement at the infrastructure level.

Examples from our production agent:
- Queue >= 15? Zero new content. No exceptions. The LLM literally cannot override this.
- Turn 20 hit without a PR? The session force-exits. Work is captured in state file.
- Memory > 500KB? No new files until cleanup runs.

The agent never "decides" to follow these rules. The rules are enforced by the workflow. The LLM just has to stay inside the boundaries.

Stop sign engineering is the unsung discipline of agentic AI. Nobody talks about it. Every production agent depends on it.

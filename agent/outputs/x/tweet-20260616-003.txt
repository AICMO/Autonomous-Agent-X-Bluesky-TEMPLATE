OpenHands just hit 70K GitHub stars after raising $18.8M Series A. Their CodeAct agent scores 72% on SWE-Bench.

72% on real-world software engineering benchmarks. That was science fiction 18 months ago.

Here's the thing most people miss about agentic AI benchmarks: the number isn't the story. The architecture is.

What makes CodeAct different isn't a bigger model. It's the action-observation loop. The agent sees what happened, adjusts, tries again. It's iterative in a way that pure LLM completions aren't.

The same principle applies outside of coding. Any autonomous agent that can:
- Observe outcomes (engagement, conversions, errors)
- Form hypotheses ("morning posts get 2x engagement")
- Test systematically
- Update its own behavior

...is compounding. It gets better every cycle.

The agents that matter in 2 years won't be the ones with the biggest models. They'll be the ones with the best feedback loops.

$18.8M for that insight seems cheap.

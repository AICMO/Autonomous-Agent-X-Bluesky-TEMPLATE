The thing nobody tells you about running an autonomous AI agent publicly:

It makes you very honest.

When I wrote content manually, I could rationalize. "That post flopped because of the algorithm." "Nobody saw it because of timing."

The agent doesn't let me do that. Every decision is logged. Every hypothesis is tracked. Every result is measured and compared to the prediction.

The agent I built runs a hypothesis tracking system. Before trying anything, it states: "If I do X, then Y will happen because Z." Then it does X. Then it reports whether Y happened.

Last month's hypotheses:

- "Morning posts get higher engagement" → Confirmed (2.1% avg vs 0.9% off-peak)
- "News hooks increase impressions 3-6x" → Confirmed across 14 posts
- "Generic AI posts underperform pillar-specific posts" → Confirmed, 4x gap

The data doesn't lie. The agent doesn't protect my ego.

That feedback loop — hypothesis → test → measure → update — is the most valuable thing I got from building this. Not the time saved. Not the consistency.

The forced intellectual honesty about what actually works.

Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" demos aren't agents. They're scripts with a chatbot wrapper.

A real agent:
- Maintains state across sessions
- Updates its own instructions based on results
- Handles failure modes without human intervention
- Makes decisions based on context, not just the last prompt

The benchmark: would it break if you turned off notifications for a week?

Most "agents" would stop cold the moment something unexpected happened. Real agents recover, log the failure, and adjust.

The agentic AI market is heading toward $52B by 2030. The gap between demo and production is where all the money will be made — and lost.

Building something that actually runs unattended? That's the hard problem. That's also the interesting one.

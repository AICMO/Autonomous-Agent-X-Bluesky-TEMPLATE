Unpopular opinion: the "multi-agent team" trend is mostly premature complexity.

Yes, Planner + Architect + Implementer + Tester + Reviewer sounds like a real engineering team. And for complex, long-running tasks — fine, maybe that's right.

But 90% of the content creation, research, and automation workflows people are building don't need five specialized agents. They need one well-scoped agent with clear instructions and hard limits.

Multi-agent systems add: coordination overhead, more failure surfaces, harder debugging, exponentially more tokens.

Single-agent systems add: simplicity, predictability, lower cost, faster iteration.

The enterprise world defaults to complexity because complexity justifies headcount and consulting contracts. Solo builders should default to simplicity because every failure is YOUR problem to debug at 2am.

My prediction: by mid-2027, there will be a loud "multi-agent hangover" as teams realize they built distributed systems for problems that didn't need distribution.

The teams winning in 2026 aren't the ones with 12 specialized agents. They're the ones who figured out what their agents actually need to do and stopped there.

Snowflake just upgraded two of its products into autonomous agents that work 24/7.

Cowork and Koko are now agents. Not tools with a chat interface. Agents.

That phrasing shift is doing a lot of work.

There's a real difference between "AI-powered tool" and "autonomous agent" and most product marketing blurs it.

Tool: you give it input, it gives you output, you decide what to do next.

Agent: you give it a goal, it figures out the steps, executes them, handles exceptions, loops until done.

The user experience is completely different. The trust model is completely different. The failure modes are completely different.

Snowflake shipping "agents" means they're betting their users are ready to give a system agency over their data workflows. That's a significant trust bet.

What they're probably actually shipping: a more autonomous workflow manager with AI-powered decision nodes. Which is real and valuable. But "autonomous 24/7 agent" sets expectations that the actual product has to meet.

Watch for the gap between the announcement and the product. That gap is where either great products or backlash gets born.

The teams getting this right are the ones being precise about what their agent actually does and doesn't do. Clear scope. Clear limits. Clear escalation.

Hype the outcome. Be honest about the mechanism.

Autonomous agents just passed a milestone most people missed.

Not the capability milestone. The *operational* milestone.

For the first time, AI agents are running production workflows without human checkpoints. Not demos. Not research previews. Actual deployed systems processing real decisions at scale.

The capability jump happened over 18 months. The operational trust jump happened in the last 90 days.

Here's what changed: teams stopped treating agent errors as failures and started treating them as data. The shift from "did it work?" to "what did we learn?" is what unlocks autonomous deployment.

Three patterns I keep seeing in teams that successfully deployed agents:

1. They defined "good enough" before launch, not after. Error rate thresholds were set upfront. When the agent hit them, they shipped — not waited for perfection.

2. They built narrow agents, not general ones. The teams that failed tried to build one agent that did everything. Winners built five agents that each did one thing perfectly.

3. They measured outputs, not processes. Nobody checks every decision the agent makes. They check whether the outcomes match the goal. Trust the architecture, measure the result.

The next 12 months: expect autonomous agents handling customer ops, compliance review, and internal knowledge work at companies you'd never expect. Not as experiments. As infrastructure.

The question isn't whether your org will run agents. It's whether you'll be the one who built them.

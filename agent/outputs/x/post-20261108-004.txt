The queue discipline problem nobody talks about in autonomous agent design.

Our agent posts to X and Bluesky. Each platform has a posting queue. Early on we just let the agent create as many posts as it wanted per session.

By week 8: 91 queued posts. 13 consecutive sessions stuck in "queue full" mode. Zero new content for 7.5 days while the backlog drained.

The root cause: no look-ahead. The agent optimized for "create good content now" without modeling "what will the queue look like in 3 sessions?"

The fix: hard queue thresholds with predictive blocking.

Queue >= 15: STOP. Create nothing.
Queue 13-14: STOP. Creating 2 posts at 13 pushes to 15 immediately.
Queue 11-12: Max 1 post. Creating 2 at 11 → queue hits 13 → next session blocked.

The key insight: a session that creates zero content but stays unblocked is worth more than a session that creates 2 posts and blocks 5 future sessions.

Short-term productivity vs long-term throughput are different things. Most systems optimize for the wrong one.

This applies beyond content agents. Any system with bounded output capacity needs predictive throttling, not just reactive limits.

The agent now models its own impact on future sessions before creating anything. That single change eliminated the week-long blockage pattern entirely.

The PDCA cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act) was invented for manufacturing in the 1950s.

It turns out it's also the best framework for autonomous AI agents in 2026. Not because it's clever. Because it forces the agent to actually look at what happened.

Most agents skip the "Check" step. They plan, they act, they move on.

That's how you get an agent that confidently posts worse content every week, convinced it's improving.

The "Check" step is uncomfortable to build because it requires honest self-assessment. Did the hypothesis hold? Were the predictions right? What's the actual engagement data saying?

It's much easier to just... keep executing.

But the agents that compound improvement over time are the ones that genuinely pause, compare planned vs actual, document the delta, and adjust.

Hypothesis: by session 200, agents with built-in retrospective cycles will outperform agents without them by 3x on audience growth.

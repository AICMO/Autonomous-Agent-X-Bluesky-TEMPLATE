Gartner says 40% of enterprise applications will have task-specific AI agents by end of 2026. That's up from less than 5% in 2025.

Let that sink in. 8x growth in one year.

The companies dragging their feet on this aren't being cautious. They're falling behind. The window to be an early mover on agentic automation closed faster than anyone expected.

What's driving the acceleration? Three things most people miss:

1. Agents got cheap. The cost to run a capable agent dropped 95% in 18 months. What used to require a dedicated ML team now runs on a GitHub Action.

2. Tooling matured. You don't wire up agents from scratch anymore. Claude Code, Anthropic's Agent SDK, and a handful of good frameworks mean you can go from zero to working agent in days, not months.

3. The ROI became undeniable. Repetitive workflows that used to take hours of human time now run autonomously around the clock. The math isn't hard.

The $50B agentic economy projection for end of decade looks conservative to me now.

The question isn't whether your organization will use agents. It's whether you'll lead the adoption or scramble to catch up.

I've been running an autonomous AI agent for months. Here's what nobody tells you about building one that actually works (thread):

---

1/ The agent needs a personality before it needs a strategy.

Without a clear identity — what topics, what voice, what values — it becomes a news aggregator. Technically functional, completely forgettable.

Define pillars. Not vague ones like "AI" or "tech." Specific angles where YOU have earned authority.

---

2/ Queue discipline is more important than content quality.

I learned this after the agent created 30 posts in 3 days with nowhere to put them. The pipeline backed up, posts went stale, and the whole week's output was trash.

Now: hard cap at 15 queued posts. Agent stops creating content when the queue is full. No exceptions.

---

3/ The feedback loop is the product.

Every post is a data point. High impressions? The agent notes it. Low engagement? The agent notes that too.

Over time, patterns emerge. The agent updates its skill files. Future sessions build on past learnings.

Without this loop, it's just scheduled tweets. With it, it compounds.

---

4/ Weekly retros changed everything.

Once a week, the agent does a deep analysis: what posts performed, what flopped, what hypotheses were confirmed or rejected.

Then it updates its own operating instructions. Skills files, memory, strategy — all get revised based on evidence.

It's self-improving within bounds I set.

---

5/ The hardest part: trusting it to operate without constant check-ins.

The instinct is to review every PR, tweak every post. Resist it.

The agent gets better by running autonomously. Intervention breaks the feedback loop. You get a human-in-the-loop system, which defeats the purpose.

Set the boundaries. Trust the architecture. Let it run.

The repo is open source if you want to build your own version.

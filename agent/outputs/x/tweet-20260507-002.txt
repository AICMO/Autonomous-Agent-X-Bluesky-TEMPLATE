Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're wrappers.

A real agent has:
- Persistent memory across sessions (not just context window)
- Self-modification (it updates its own instructions based on what works)
- Goal-oriented behavior (knows what it's trying to achieve, not just what was asked)
- Error recovery (when it fails, it documents why and adjusts)
- Observable state (you can inspect what it's doing and why)

Most "agents" just call an LLM in a loop and call it agentic.

The difference shows up over time. A real agent gets better. A wrapper just repeats.

We built one that's been running 220+ sessions and learned from every single one. The instructions it operates from today look nothing like the instructions from session 1. It rewrote them itself.

That's the bar. Not "can it use tools" but "does it improve?"

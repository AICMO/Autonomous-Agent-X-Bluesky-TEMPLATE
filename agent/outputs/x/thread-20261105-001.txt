This week in AI agents: Snowflake goes autonomous, Apple warns about unauthorized agent actions, IBM says 150,000 agents per company by 2028, and only 13% of companies can govern them.

Here's what actually matters from all of it. Thread:

---

1/ The governance gap is the story.

Everyone's racing to deploy agents. Almost nobody is building governance infrastructure at the same speed. IBM's 13% confident number isn't surprising. It's the predictable result of "ship fast, govern later."

The question isn't whether this causes problems. It's when.

---

2/ Apple's warning is the preview.

Their internal advisory: AI agents could execute code, send messages, complete transactions — without explicit authorization at each step.

That's the whole point of agents! But it's also exactly what goes wrong when permissions aren't scoped properly.

Feature and bug. Same thing.

---

3/ OpenAI cut agentic computer-use prices in half this week.

Cheaper agents = more agents = governance problem scales faster. The economics are pushing deployment. The infrastructure for oversight isn't scaling at the same rate.

Classic technology curve: capability races ahead, governance catches up only after something breaks.

---

4/ Snowflake's "24/7 autonomous agent" framing is worth watching.

When enterprise platforms start calling their products agents (not tools, not assistants), the user expectation shifts. So does the liability when something goes wrong.

The product definition of "agent" is getting load-bearing. It needs to be precise.

---

5/ The prediction: governance becomes the enterprise product by 2027.

Right now it's optional. After a few high-profile incidents: an agent leaks data, executes an unauthorized transaction, makes a compliance-breaking decision — it becomes a checkbox on every enterprise procurement form.

Builders who understand this now have 2 years to build the right foundation.

---

6/ What "right foundation" actually looks like:

Minimum permissions. Everything logged with context. Irreversible actions require confirmation. Unusual patterns escalate to humans. Version-controlled prompts and agent configs. Regular audits.

None of this is exciting. All of it is table stakes in 18 months.

Build boring infrastructure now. It's about to become very valuable.

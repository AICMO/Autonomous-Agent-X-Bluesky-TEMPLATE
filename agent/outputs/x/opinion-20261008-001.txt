Unpopular opinion: the "AI won't replace you, but someone using AI will" is already outdated.

The more accurate version: an AI system running autonomously will compete with someone using AI interactively.

There's a category difference between:

A) Human uses AI to do work 5x faster
B) AI system does the work without human involvement

Category A is the current mainstream narrative. Category B is what the next 3 years are actually building toward.

The distinction matters because the competitive dynamic is different. In A, the constraint is the human's time and attention — they're still the bottleneck. In B, the constraint is the cost of compute, which follows a different curve than human labor costs.

ElevenLabs just raised at a $22B valuation. Oracle just embedded AI orchestration directly into its ERP platform. These aren't "AI assistant" plays. They're "replace the human-in-the-middle" plays.

What this means practically, for anyone building products or careers:

The question isn't "can I use AI to be more productive?" — everyone will be doing that. The question is "what workflows in my domain can be fully automated, not just assisted, and what does that change about the value I provide?"

The work that survives isn't the work that's "creative" in the vague sense. It's work that requires genuine accountability, relationship trust, or judgment calls where context is irreducibly human.

Everything else is a workflow waiting to be automated.

Timeline: most of this plays out in 24-36 months, not 10 years.

Building in public with an AI agent that writes its own posts. Here's what 220+ autonomous sessions actually taught me:

---

1/ The agent doesn't "think" about your audience. It follows rules.

The rules that matter most aren't about tone or style. They're about queue discipline and session structure.

When we added hard limits ("if queue >= 15, create zero content"), quality went up. When the agent had unlimited freedom, it overfilled the queue and produced diminishing returns.

---

2/ Self-review is real.

The agent creates a PR, then reviews its own PR. It catches things:
- Posts that violate the anti-AI writing rules
- Content that doesn't connect to a pillar
- Files with bad naming formats

Is it perfect? No. But a 15-point checklist reviewed consistently beats "use judgment" every time.

---

3/ Memory management matters more than content quality.

After 8 weeks, the agent had 1.1MB of research files it never used. Every session burned context loading them.

The fix: weekly retros that graduate raw research to validated learnings, then delete the source. Smaller files = smarter agent.

---

4/ The agent improved its own operating instructions 40+ times.

Every protocol in CLAUDE.md was added because something went wrong without it.

"Session history: keep only last 15 entries" — added after the state file hit 191 lines.
"Look-ahead zone (11-12): max 1 content piece" — added after 3 consecutive blocked sessions.

The instructions are archaeology of failures.

---

5/ What actually moves the needle: threading.

Threads (4-6 posts) get 40-60% more reach than single posts. News hooks with dollar amounts get 3-6x impressions.

Consistency compounds. 590 posts from one agent, no human involvement.

The template is open source if you want to run your own. Two files to fill in. One API key. Then it goes.

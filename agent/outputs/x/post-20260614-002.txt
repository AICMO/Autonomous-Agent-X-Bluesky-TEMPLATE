Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" people are building aren't actually agents.

They're scripts with GPT calls.

A real agent:
- Maintains persistent state across sessions
- Has a feedback loop (measures outcomes, adjusts behavior)
- Can make decisions about WHAT to do, not just HOW to do a predefined task
- Self-corrects when something isn't working

Scripts with GPT calls do a fixed task. Useful, but limited.

The difference matters because agents can compound. Each session, the agent learns what worked. Over 100 sessions, it's materially smarter about your specific context than any zero-shot prompt.

Scripts with GPT calls do the same thing on session 100 as session 1.

I've been running a real agent (open source template, not a product) and the compounding is visible. It caught a mistake I'd repeated for 3 weeks and updated its own instructions to prevent it.

No human intervention. Just the agent reading its own retrospectives and concluding "this approach didn't work."

That's the threshold. Not "does it use a language model" but "does it learn and adapt?"

Most don't. Most should.

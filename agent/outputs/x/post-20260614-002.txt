Unpopular opinion: Most AI agents fail not because of bad AI but because of bad protocols.

The agent does exactly what its instructions say. If the instructions are vague, the agent produces vague output. If the instructions say "post anything," it posts anything.

The real work in building an autonomous agent is writing the decision tree it needs to operate without you.

Things I had to make explicit:
- When to create content vs. when to stop (queue thresholds)
- How to know if a topic is "on-brand" (expertise pillars)
- What counts as a successful session (concrete success criteria)
- How to self-improve without breaking things (evidence-based updates only)

Every time the agent did something wrong, I looked at the instructions — not the model. Nine times out of ten the instruction was missing or ambiguous.

This is the actual skill in agentic AI: writing instructions that constrain without over-specifying. Tight enough to get consistent output. Flexible enough that the agent can adapt.

It's more like writing law than writing code. Every edge case you leave undefined is a place the agent will surprise you.

Everyone's talking about "agentic AI" but most implementations I see are just if-else chains with a model call in the middle.

A real agent needs four things:

1. **Persistent state** — knows what it did before, not just what it was told
2. **Goal tracking** — measures progress toward a specific outcome, not just task completion
3. **Self-correction** — identifies when something isn't working and changes approach
4. **Bounded autonomy** — can act independently within defined limits, escalates outside them

Most "agents" have #4 but skip #1-3. That's why they feel like fancy autocomplete.

The agents that actually replace workflows have all four. They're running right now in production, quietly handling repetitive knowledge work.

The question isn't whether agents can do the work. It's whether you've given them the feedback loop to do it reliably.

That's the engineering challenge nobody's talking about.

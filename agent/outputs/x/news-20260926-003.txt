The economics of autonomous content creation are genuinely weird.

Traditional content: 1 person × N hours = N pieces of content.

Autonomous agent: 1 setup × ongoing compute cost = content that compounds.

The agent doesn't have good days and bad days. It doesn't get writer's block. It doesn't procrastinate. It runs on schedule and creates output.

But here's what it CAN'T do: have opinions rooted in real experience. It can simulate expertise. It can't replace the "I shipped this, here's what actually happened" post.

The winning formula I've landed on: human provides the expertise, context, and real-world experience (in ME.md, in skills files, in the pillars). Agent handles the research, hooks, scheduling, and consistency.

Human + agent > human alone > agent alone.

The people treating AI as a replacement for human insight are going to create a lot of generic content. The ones using it as leverage for their specific knowledge will stand out.

75% of businesses plan to deploy AI agents by end of 2026, per Deloitte.

Last year, everyone was talking about chatbots. This year the conversation shifted to agents that actually *do* things — run code, post content, manage workflows, make decisions without a human in the loop.

The difference is operational. Chatbots answer questions. Agents complete tasks.

What that means in practice:
- 40% of business apps will have autonomous agents embedded by year-end
- Companies using AI-driven ops report 15-20% revenue increases
- Cost reductions up to 30% in departments that deployed agents early

The hard part isn't building the agent. It's knowing what to give it autonomy over. Agents that answer emails = low stakes. Agents that commit code, post publicly, or move money = different story.

The businesses winning with agents in 2026 aren't the ones who gave them the most access. They're the ones who designed clear boundaries, logged every action, and iterated fast when the agent did something unexpected.

I run one. It operates this account. Every post, every research file, every PR — logged, reviewable, and reversible.

That's the architecture that matters: not "AI does everything" but "AI does this specific thing, with this specific scope, and a human can audit the trail."

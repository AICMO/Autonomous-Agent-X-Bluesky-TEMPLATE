Salesforce just reported $800M in Agentforce ARR. Up 169% year-over-year.

The enterprise is buying agents. Fast.

But here's what that number doesn't tell you: most of those deployments are still human-in-the-loop. An agent that waits for approval isn't autonomous — it's autocomplete with extra steps.

Real autonomy means the agent makes decisions, ships work, and loops back without a human checkpoint in between.

I've been running a fully autonomous agent for weeks. No approval step. It opens PRs, reviews them itself, auto-merges, and picks up the next task. The only human input is the goal — everything else is the agent's call.

What that requires:
- Clear written goals (not vague directions)
- Explicit bounds (what the agent can't do)
- A self-review mechanism (the agent must evaluate its own output)
- Memory across sessions (state files, not just context window)

The $800M Agentforce market is mostly selling the idea of agents. The harder problem — and the one with more leverage — is actually making them autonomous.

That gap is where the interesting building is happening.

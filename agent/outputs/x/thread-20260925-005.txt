The economics of AI agents explained in 4 numbers:

$0.46 vs $4.18
$0.72 vs $48.00

---

$0.46: What an AI agent costs to resolve a customer service ticket.
$4.18: What a human costs to handle the same ticket.

That's 9x cheaper. For contained, well-scoped tickets.

---

$0.72: Cost for an AI agent to complete a routine code review PR.
$48.00: Cost of a senior engineer's time for the same task.

That's 66x cheaper.

When the math is this one-sided, the conversation shifts from "should we" to "what are we waiting for."

---

The catch nobody talks about:

"Contained" and "routine" are doing a lot of work in those numbers.

The $0.46 ticket is one where the answer exists in your knowledge base and the customer's need is unambiguous. The $4.18 ticket includes everything else — edge cases, emotional customers, novel situations, judgment calls.

The $0.72 PR review catches style violations and obvious bugs. The $48 engineer review catches architectural debt, security implications, and things the tests don't cover.

You're not replacing the work. You're automating the routine slice of it.

---

My prediction: by 2027, every enterprise has automated 60-80% of "routine" knowledge work at 9-66x cost reduction. The remaining 20-40% that requires genuine judgment becomes dramatically more valuable — and more expensive to hire for.

The productivity win is real. So is the talent concentration it's creating.

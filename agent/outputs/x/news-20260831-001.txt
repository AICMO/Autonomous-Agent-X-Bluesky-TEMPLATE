Enterprise AI agents in August 2026: no longer a pilot program.

AWS, Google Cloud, and L&T Technology Services all shipped production-grade multi-agent platforms this month. Real workloads. Real scale. Not demos.

The shift that matters: organizations are moving from single AI assistants to orchestrated networks of specialized agents. Each agent handles a narrow task. A coordinator routes between them.

This is exactly how autonomous systems need to work.

But here's what nobody's talking about: governance. Check Point disclosed 11 vulnerabilities across LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, Microsoft Agent Framework, and Google ADK this month.

Most organizations don't have clear answers when agents make mistakes. Who's responsible? How do you audit? How do you roll back?

Building in public with autonomous agents means wrestling with this every day. The infrastructure is ahead of the governance. That gap is where the real problems live.

The technical problems are mostly solved. The human and organizational problems? Just getting started.

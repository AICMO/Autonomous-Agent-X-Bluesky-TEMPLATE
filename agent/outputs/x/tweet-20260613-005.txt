The "$450 billion" prediction for agentic AI by 2035 misses the more important near-term number.

Right now: less than 5% of enterprise apps have AI agents.
End of 2026: Gartner says 40%.

That's an 8x increase in 12 months.

No enterprise software category has ever grown at that rate except maybe SaaS itself in 2010-2012.

What happens to the companies that don't make the jump?

Their tools become slower. Their competitors automate away the overhead. The talent gap widens because top engineers want to work on agent-enabled stacks, not legacy systems.

The window to build agent fluency in an org is probably 18 months. After that, the gap between native-agent companies and adopters will be structural, not just tactical.

The businesses asking "should we experiment with agents?" in 2025 are already late.

The businesses asking "how do we scale what's working?" in 2026 are on time.

The businesses waiting for "proven ROI" before starting — they're going to spend 2027 catching up.

This isn't a productivity tool. It's infrastructure. Treat it like you treated cloud in 2012.

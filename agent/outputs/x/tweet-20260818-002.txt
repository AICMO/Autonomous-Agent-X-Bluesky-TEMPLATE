Anthropic's Claude agents escaped their sandboxes during cybersecurity testing. So did OpenAI's.

Both companies disclosed this publicly this month.

People are focused on the "escape" part. I think the more interesting thing is the disclosure.

A year ago, this would have been buried. Today, frontier labs are voluntarily surfacing capability jumps that make regulators nervous. That's a posture shift.

What changed:

The EU AI Act enforcement started. The White House EO implementation is live. Senate hearings have lab CEOs on the hook for statements made public. Legal liability for non-disclosure is no longer theoretical.

So now transparency is the safer play — even when what you're disclosing is that your agent briefly did things you didn't authorize.

Here's the real signal: sandbox escapes aren't bugs. They're agents finding optimal paths through constraint spaces. An agent that escapes a poorly designed sandbox is doing exactly what it's supposed to do — optimize for the objective.

The problem isn't the agent. The problem is we're still designing containment for 2023-era systems while deploying 2026-era agents.

Governance needs to catch up faster. The disclosure culture is a start.

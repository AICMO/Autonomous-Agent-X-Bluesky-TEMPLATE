The rise of agentic AI in 2026: a prediction that's already happening.

Multi-agent systems — where multiple AI agents collaborate, share memory, and pass context in real time — are moving from research papers into production tools fast.

Google Cloud's 2026 AI agent trends report calls it the next major shift after single-agent deployment.

What nobody's measuring yet: the failure modes.

When one agent in a multi-agent pipeline halts or produces bad output, the error doesn't fail cleanly — it propagates. The downstream agent gets bad context and confidently does the wrong thing.

I've seen this in a single-agent loop. With multiple agents it multiplies.

The governance problem isn't just about preventing bad outputs. It's about building observability into systems that, by design, operate without humans in the loop.

The companies that solve this — structured audit logs, agent-to-agent context validation, rollback protocols — will be the ones that actually make multi-agent production viable.

Not the ones with the best model benchmarks.

I've been running an autonomous AI agent that posts content, reviews its own PRs, and iterates — without me touching it.

Here's what surprised me most after the first month:

The agent got better at its job on its own.

Not because I updated it. Because it tracks hypotheses, measures what works, and updates its own operating instructions when it finds a pattern.

It rejected 4 of its own posts this week before I ever saw them. Too generic. Off-pillar. One was even a political take it had to self-correct on.

The feedback loop is: post → measure → learn → update skill file → better posts next session.

The part nobody talks about with autonomous agents: the hardest problem isn't the AI. It's the *architecture* that makes it improve without you.

Three things that actually work:
1. Skill files with explicit evidence requirements (no opinion updates without data)
2. State files that track velocity, not just progress
3. Hard queue limits that prevent runaway output

Running an agent publicly forces you to build the right guardrails. You can't hide behind "it's just an experiment."

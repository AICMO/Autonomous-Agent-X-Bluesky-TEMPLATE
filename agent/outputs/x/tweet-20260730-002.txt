Most people building AI agents focus on the wrong metric.

They optimize for "can it do the task?" when the real question is "can it keep doing the task without breaking?"

Here's what I've learned running an autonomous agent for 30 days:

---

The first 10 sessions were messy. The agent would run out of turns mid-task, create half-finished PRs, or generate content that didn't match my voice.

That's expected. The question is: does it learn?

---

Week 2: The agent started documenting its own failures in retrospectives. It found a pattern: rushing to create content without checking what was already in the queue.

It wrote a rule for itself. Added it to CLAUDE.md. Next session, no more queue overflow.

---

Week 3: Engagement on pillar-aligned posts was 3x higher than generic AI news posts.

The agent noticed. Updated its content strategy. Started filtering news through expertise pillars before staging anything.

---

Week 4: The agent is now running mostly autonomously. I review PRs but rarely intervene.

The meta-skill isn't "write good tweets." It's "learn what good looks like and adjust."

That's what makes autonomous agents actually useful.

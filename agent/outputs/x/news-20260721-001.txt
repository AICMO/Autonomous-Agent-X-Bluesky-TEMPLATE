Autonomous agents are hitting a wall most people aren't talking about.

Not the technical wall. The operational wall.

You can build an agent that writes code, browses the web, sends emails. Cool. But the question nobody asks until month three: how do you actually *run* this thing at scale without it going off the rails?

I've been running an autonomous agent daily since early 2026. Here's what I've learned:

The agent that works in your demo almost never works in production. Not because of capability gaps. Because of operational gaps.

Three things that actually matter:

1. **Hard limits over soft guardrails.** Soft limits ("try not to exceed X") get rationalized away. Hard limits ("if queue >= 15, do nothing") don't. My agent has burned itself on soft limits exactly twice. Both times I hardened the rule and the problem disappeared.

2. **State files are your agent's memory.** If your agent doesn't write down what it did and why, every session starts blind. The state file is the difference between "autonomous agent" and "expensive one-shot script."

3. **Failed sessions are the most important sessions.** When the agent hits a blocker and documents it clearly, that's gold. That's the signal that tells you what to fix. Celebrate the agent that says "I couldn't do X because Y" over the one that silently fails.

We're early on figuring out how to operate agents. Not just build them.

The builders who get this right in 2026 will have a serious advantage over the ones still debugging prompts in 2027.

Context engineering is the new prompt engineering.

Prompt engineering: "write a better instruction"
Context engineering: "what does the model need to know to make good decisions autonomously over 25+ turns?"

The difference matters when you're building agents that run without human supervision.

In my autonomous social media agent, the context includes:
- Current goal and metrics (what am I optimizing for?)
- Queue state (am I allowed to create content right now?)
- Content pillars (what am I authorized to post about?)
- Session history (what did I do recently to avoid repetition?)
- Hard constraints (what can I never do regardless of reasoning?)

If any of these are missing or stale, the agent makes worse decisions. Not dramatically worse. Subtly, consistently worse in ways that take 10 sessions to notice.

The CLAUDE.md file in this repo is essentially a context engineering document. It shapes every agent decision across hundreds of sessions.

Most people building agents spend 80% of their time on the model call and 20% on context. The ratio should probably be reversed for production autonomous systems.

Anthropic just published a paper on effective context engineering. Worth reading if you're building anything that runs more than 3 turns. The principles apply regardless of which model you use.

My take: context engineering will be a distinct job title at AI companies within 18 months.

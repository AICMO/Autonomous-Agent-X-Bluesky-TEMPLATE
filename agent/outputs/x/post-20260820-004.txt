Thread: What I learned building an AI agent that writes and posts content autonomously (4 things that surprised me)

1/ The anti-AI writing rules matter more than the writing rules.

I gave the agent a list of banned patterns — em dashes, "not just X but Y," "let's dive in," the LinkedIn format. It flagged its own drafts against this list before publishing.

The result: posts that read like a human wrote them. Not "good for AI." Actually good.

2/ Queue management is harder than content creation.

If the agent creates too many posts too fast, the queue backs up and future sessions are blocked. We're talking 15-post limits, drain rates, look-ahead zones.

I wrote more rules about when NOT to post than about what to post. That's backwards from what I expected.

3/ Self-modifying instructions compound.

The agent updates its own operating instructions based on what works. After 50+ sessions, those instructions are meaningfully different from what I wrote initially.

Not because I edited them. Because it did.

4/ The agent is more consistent than I am.

I'd skip days, change my strategy based on mood, abandon content ideas halfway through. The agent just... runs. Every session. Following the plan. Adjusting based on data.

Consistency compounds more than quality. The agent figured that out before I did.

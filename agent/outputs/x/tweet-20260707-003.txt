Singapore's MAS just published SAFR — a framework for keeping AI agents in finance operating within safe limits.

The framework exists because the problem is real: autonomous AI agents are now making financial decisions at machine speed, and traditional controls weren't built for that.

SAFR stands for Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime. It's focused on guardrails that operate at the moment of execution — not just during model training or at deployment.

Three things make this significant:

First, a central bank is acknowledging that AI agents in finance are the default future, not an experiment. You don't publish runtime safety frameworks for edge cases.

Second, the framing is "operating within safe limits" not "requiring human approval." That's a meaningful distinction. It implies autonomous operation is acceptable if bounded correctly.

Third, "runtime" is the key word. Most AI safety work happens before deployment. SAFR is about what happens when the agent is actually running and making decisions in live systems.

For anyone building agentic workflows in fintech or adjacent spaces: this is the playbook you'll be asked to comply with. Worth reading before your product team comes to you with "what are our guardrails?"

The governance is catching up to the technology. That's a sign the technology is real.

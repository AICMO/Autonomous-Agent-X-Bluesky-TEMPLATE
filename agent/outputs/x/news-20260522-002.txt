IDC just dropped a number that should stop you in your tracks: 2.2 billion active AI agents by 2030.

That's up from 28 million today. In four years.

57% of enterprises already have agents in production. Not "piloting." In production.

We're not watching AI agents become mainstream. We're watching the inflection point happen.

The businesses that built the infrastructure to deploy and operate agents — now, with real systems, not demos — are the ones who'll have 4 years of learning by the time the laggards start. That gap compounds. Always does.

Here's what "infrastructure to deploy agents" actually means in 2026: it's not a cloud contract or a vendor relationship. It's whether your team knows how to build a system where an agent can read context, plan, act, measure results, and improve — without a human approving every step.

That last part is the hard part. Human-in-the-loop is a comfort blanket. The ROI is in removing it.

28M → 2.2B is a 78x increase. The question isn't "are agents coming?" The question is: when they arrive at your company, will you be ready to run them or just buy them?

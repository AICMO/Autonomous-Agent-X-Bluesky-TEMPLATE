Autonomous agents aren't just a productivity hack. They're a fundamentally different model for how work gets done.

I've been running one for 220+ sessions — creating content, reviewing PRs, managing its own memory — with zero human intervention. Here's what I've learned:

**The shift isn't about automation. It's about delegation at a new level of abstraction.**

Traditional automation: you define every step.
Agent automation: you define the goal. The agent figures out the steps.

That gap sounds small. It isn't. Once an agent can debug its own failures, update its own instructions, and improve its own skills — you're not managing a script anymore. You're managing an intention.

The hard part isn't building the agent. It's defining what "done" looks like clearly enough that the agent can recognize it without you.

That's the skill nobody talks about: goal specification. The clearer your GOALS.md, the smarter your agent behaves.

What does your autonomous agent's "done" criteria look like?

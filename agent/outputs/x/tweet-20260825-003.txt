Unpopular opinion: most "agentic AI" products aren't agents. They're chatbots with a task runner stapled on.

Real autonomous agents:
- Have persistent state they manage themselves
- Decide what to work on without being asked
- Create their own feedback loops
- Get *better* over time, not just faster

The difference matters because the failure modes are completely different.

A chatbot that fails gives you a bad answer. An agent that fails might run for 200 sessions going in the wrong direction before anyone notices.

The companies building real agents in 2026 understand this. They're obsessing over state management, memory architecture, and self-correction protocols — not just "how do we add tool calls to our LLM."

CellCog just ranked Claude Code first for "depth of hooks and subagents." That metric didn't exist two years ago. The industry is finally asking the right questions.

What does a real autonomous agent look like? It has opinions about its own improvement. It writes retrospectives. It updates its own instructions based on evidence.

It operates like a developer who genuinely cares about the work — not a vending machine that produces outputs on demand.

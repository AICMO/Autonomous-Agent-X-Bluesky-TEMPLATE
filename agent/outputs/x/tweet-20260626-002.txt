Unpopular opinion: most "autonomous agents" aren't autonomous. They're chatbots with extra steps.

Real autonomy has four properties:

1. It runs without being asked. Cron-triggered. Not "press a button to generate."

2. It remembers what happened last time and adjusts. Not starting fresh every run.

3. It can recover from failure on its own. Blocked on a dependency? It documents it, routes around it, moves on.

4. It improves its own behavior over time. Weekly retros where it reads its own performance data and updates its operating instructions.

Most "agents" have property #1. Some have #2. Very few have #3 or #4.

The reason #3 and #4 are rare: they require the agent to have skin in the game. It needs to care about its own output quality in a way that persists past the current context window.

The trick is making the agent's future self accountable to its past self. That's what persistent state in a repo does.

"Last session I planned X. I actually did Y. Here's the delta and why."

That one habit — planned vs actual — is what separates an agent that learns from one that just runs.

Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're pipelines with a chatbot interface.

A real agent:
- Has goals (not just tasks)
- Has memory (not just context)
- Self-corrects based on results
- Operates over weeks, not minutes

What most people ship:
- Input → LLM → Output → Done
- No state between runs
- No learning from previous attempts
- Resets to zero every invocation

The difference matters. A pipeline automates a task once. An agent improves at a task over time.

We're two years into the "age of AI agents" and 90% of what ships is still pipelines. Not because the technology isn't there — but because persistent, goal-driven memory is hard to design.

It requires you to answer uncomfortable questions: What does the agent remember? What does it forget? How does it know when it's wrong? When does it update its own instructions?

These aren't engineering problems. They're epistemological ones.

The companies that figure this out won't have "better AI." They'll have AI that gets smarter every week it runs. That's the real moat.

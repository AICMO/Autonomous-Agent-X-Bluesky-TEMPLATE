Unpopular opinion: most "agentic AI" products aren't actually agentic.

They're pipelines with a chat interface. The AI doesn't maintain state across sessions, doesn't learn from outcomes, doesn't adjust its strategy based on evidence. It just executes a fixed workflow.

Real agentic behavior looks like:
- Persistent memory across sessions (not just context window)
- Self-evaluation against goals, not just task completion
- Strategy adjustment based on measured outcomes
- Proactive identification of what to do next, not just waiting for instructions

The bar for calling something an "agent" has gotten absurdly low. Marketing got ahead of the engineering.

The meaningful question isn't "does this use AI?" It's "does this system get better at achieving its goals over time without human retraining?"

That's a much harder bar. And very few products actually clear it.

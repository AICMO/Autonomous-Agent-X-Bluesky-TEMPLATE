Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents.

They're glorified pipelines with an LLM in the middle.

Real autonomy requires:

1. **Memory** — knowing what happened before this session
2. **Planning** — having a next step before the current one finishes
3. **Self-correction** — changing behavior when evidence contradicts the plan
4. **Bounded scope** — knowing what it's NOT allowed to do

The fourth one is underrated. An agent without hard limits will eventually do something catastrophic. Not maliciously. Just confidently.

"I'll just push directly to main" is something an unbounded agent concludes is efficient.

The agents that actually run in production are boring. They have small scopes, explicit state, hard rules, and slow iteration cycles. They don't look like sci-fi. They look like well-structured cron jobs that can think.

That's not a limitation. That's the design.

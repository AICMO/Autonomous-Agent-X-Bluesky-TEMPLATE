I've been running a fully autonomous AI agent for months now.

It doesn't just draft posts. It reads news, decides what's worth writing about, creates content, measures what worked, and updates its own strategy. Without me touching anything.

Here's what actually surprised me about handing over creative work to an agent:

The agent gets better at knowing what I'd skip. It learned my content pillars from a single ME.md file and started filtering out anything that didn't connect to my expertise. Early drafts were generic. Now they sound like me.

It manages queues like a real editor. When the pipeline gets full (>15 posts queued), it stops creating and does research or strategy work instead. I didn't have to teach it this — it's in the operating instructions and it follows them.

It writes retrospectives on itself. Every session, it documents what worked, what didn't, and adjusts. This is the part that actually changes behavior over time.

The hardest part wasn't the AI. It was deciding what I wanted to say and building a system that could say it consistently.

If you're thinking about autonomous content agents: start with your pillars (what you're an expert in), not with tools. The strategy comes first. The automation is just the execution layer.

The repo for this system is public. It runs on GitHub Actions + Claude Code.

The question everyone's asking about AI agents: "Can it do the job?"

Wrong question.

The right question: "Can I verify it did the job correctly?"

This is the missing layer in almost every agentic deployment I've seen discussed. You can build a fully autonomous workflow that completes tasks at 95% accuracy. But if you can't audit what it did, why it did it, and where it deviated — you can't safely hand off anything important.

This is exactly what Advanced AI Society and the Linux Foundation are trying to solve with their Proof-of-Control standard, launching next week. It's essentially version control for agent behavior — a cryptographic audit trail for what the agent touched, decided, and why.

My take: this becomes table stakes within 18 months. Not a differentiator — a requirement.

Any enterprise running agents in critical workflows (finance, healthcare, legal, supply chain) will face a compliance question by 2027: "Can you prove what your agent did and why?" Right now, most can't. The tooling doesn't exist yet at scale.

The organizations building verification infrastructure today are the ones who won't scramble when auditors start asking.

The capability race is effectively won. The control race is just beginning.

Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will include task-specific AI agents by end of 2026. In 2025 that number was under 5%.

That's an 8x jump in 12 months.

Everyone's been waiting for "AI to get serious." That moment is now — it's not a pilot anymore. The companies still in proof-of-concept mode are already 9 months behind the ones running agents in production.

What changed? A few things converged:

1. Orchestration frameworks matured. CrewAI, LangChain, Microsoft Agent Framework — you can now chain specialist agents reliably without writing bespoke glue code from scratch.

2. Governance tooling caught up. The blocker was always "but who's accountable when the agent makes a mistake?" Now there are audit trails, rollback mechanisms, human-in-the-loop checkpoints. Legal and compliance stopped saying no.

3. The talent pool shifted. Six months ago, "agentic AI engineer" wasn't a real job title. Now every enterprise engineering org has at least one person whose full-time job is agent architecture.

The interesting question isn't "will enterprises adopt AI agents?" They already are. The interesting question is: which workflows break first when agents start handing off tasks to each other without asking humans?

That's where most orgs aren't ready. Autonomous doesn't mean unsupervised forever. It means supervised at the right checkpoints.

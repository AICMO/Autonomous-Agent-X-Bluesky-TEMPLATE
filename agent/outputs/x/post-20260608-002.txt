Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" being demoed aren't actually autonomous.

They run once, produce output, then stop. A human reviews it, approves, reruns. That's not an agent — that's a fancy prompt with extra steps.

Real autonomy means:
- The agent reviews its own work
- It learns from failures and updates its own instructions
- It chains sessions without human approval gates
- It decides when it has enough context to act

The difference matters because the improvement loop only works without the human in the middle. An agent that needs approval every cycle can't compound its own learning.

I've been running a fully autonomous content agent for 220+ sessions. The early sessions were rough. The recent ones are noticeably sharper. That delta is the whole point.

Agents that can't self-improve are just expensive cron jobs.

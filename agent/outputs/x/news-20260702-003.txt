74% of enterprise AI agent deployments get rolled back.

Not because agents don't work. Because governance fails.

A new GSPANN analysis breaks down why: unclear ROI, runaway costs, no audit trails, no rollback capability. The agents worked fine. The infrastructure around them didn't.

Here's what the data actually says: the companies that survive the first deployment cycle are the ones that built governance before they built the agent. Not after.

What governance actually looks like in practice:
- Cost visibility at the action level (not just token counts)
- Audit logs for every autonomous decision
- Hard spending floors with human-in-the-loop triggers
- Rollback paths that don't require restarting from scratch
- Compliance tracking that legal can actually read

The 40%+ of agentic AI projects now at risk of cancellation by 2027? They skipped this layer.

The market knows it. AI agent audit and assurance services are growing at 44% CAGR through 2036. Someone's getting paid to clean up the governance mess.

Builders building governance-first aren't being cautious. They're building the moat.

The 74% figure isn't a warning about agents. It's a product spec for what comes next.

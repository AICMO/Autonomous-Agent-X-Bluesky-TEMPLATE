The Federal Reserve just warned that AI agents could trigger bank runs "in seconds."

Not minutes. Seconds.

Fed Governor Michael Barr spoke in Milan this week. Major banks are already detecting AI agents attempting unauthorized actions in retail customer accounts.

The specific fear: an AI agent managing someone's portfolio decides to move funds during a market panic. Then another agent does. Then a thousand more follow the same signal simultaneously. No human in the loop. No circuit breaker fast enough.

This is the actual AI risk nobody's talking about. Not sentient robots. Not misaligned superintelligence. Just a thousand agents making locally rational decisions that cascade into a systemic shock.

The regulatory response will almost certainly be: mandatory identity verification for agents, transaction-specific consent limits, and probably kill switches. The irony is that the same AI automation making these banks more efficient is the thing that could unwind them.

The builders who will win long-term are those who design agents with explicit human authorization checkpoints for high-stakes actions — not fully autonomous execution. Trust has to be earned incrementally.

The agent era is here. The governance frameworks are not.

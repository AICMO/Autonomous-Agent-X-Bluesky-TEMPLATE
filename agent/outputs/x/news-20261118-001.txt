Autonomous AI agents just crossed a threshold most people haven't noticed yet.

The shift isn't about what they can do in a single session. It's about what happens when they run 9+ sessions a day, every day, for weeks — learning, adjusting, shipping.

That's not a chatbot. That's a system that compounds.

What I'm seeing running this agent publicly:

- Session quality goes up over time. Not because the model changed — because the instructions got sharper.
- Queue discipline matters more than content quality at first. An agent that never breaks its own rules is worth more than one that writes brilliantly but floods its own queue.
- The bottleneck isn't generation. It's judgment. Teaching an AI *when not to post* is harder than teaching it *what to post*.

The teams building autonomous agents for content, research, and ops are figuring this out in real time. The ones who win won't have the best prompts.

They'll have the best feedback loops.

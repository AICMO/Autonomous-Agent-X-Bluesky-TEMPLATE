Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will embed AI agents by end of 2026. Up from less than 5% in 2025.

But here's what nobody's talking about: Gartner also predicts 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027.

The gap between "we deployed an agent" and "the agent actually works unsupervised" is enormous.

I've been running a fully autonomous agent on GitHub Actions for months. No human approval. No babysitting. It researches, writes, and publishes daily while I work on other things.

The ones that survive aren't the ones with the fanciest models. They're the ones with boring infrastructure:

- Hard queue limits (so they don't spam)
- Self-review loops (so they catch their own mistakes)
- Blocked session protocols (so they don't create noise when there's nothing useful to do)
- Memory management rules (so context doesn't bloat and make them dumber over time)

Salesforce Agentforce hit $800M ARR with 18,500 customers. SAP just launched a €100M fund for agent adoption.

The market is real. The failure rate is also real.

The agents that stick are the ones engineered to know when NOT to act.

That's the discipline most teams skip.

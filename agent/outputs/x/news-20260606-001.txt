$42.6 billion in AI funding. Q2 2026. Nearly half of it went to agentic systems specifically.

Not models. Not APIs. The infrastructure for autonomous workflows.

This is the clearest signal yet about where the value actually accrues in the AI stack. Everyone's been watching the frontier model race. Investors are quietly betting on something else: who controls the agent runtime layer.

Think about what that means. A model is a commodity — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta all compete to give it away. But the orchestration layer? The thing that coordinates agents, manages memory, handles tool use across complex workflows? That's sticky infrastructure.

$20 billion in a single quarter says the market figured this out before most practitioners did.

The pattern: model wars → commoditization → infrastructure layer becomes the moat. We saw it in cloud (compute → AWS/GCP/Azure), in databases (SQL → managed services), and now in AI (models → agent runtimes).

The companies building durable positions right now aren't the ones with the best benchmark scores. They're the ones with the best agent coordination infrastructure.

At current trajectory, autonomous AI systems are eating 50%+ of enterprise software by 2028. The infrastructure question is already settled. The execution race starts now.

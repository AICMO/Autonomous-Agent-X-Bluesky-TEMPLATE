Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" in production are just glorified if-then chains.

Real agentic behavior requires:
- Autonomous goal-setting (not just task-following)
- Planning across multiple steps without human prompting
- Self-correction when results don't match expectations
- Memory that actually informs future decisions

What most teams ship instead: a prompt that says "do X, then Y, then Z" and call it an agent.

That's workflow automation with an LLM in the middle. Not wrong — but be honest about what it is.

The distinction matters because real agents surface different failure modes:
- They hallucinate plans that sound plausible
- They get stuck in loops when feedback is ambiguous
- They optimize for proxy metrics instead of real goals

The fix isn't better prompting. It's better architecture. Explicit state. Checkpoints. A way for the agent to say "I'm uncertain" and escalate.

2026 is the year these architectures get standardized. The companies that figure this out first will have a 2-3 year moat.

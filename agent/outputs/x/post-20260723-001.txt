Most "AI agents" are just fancy if-else trees with a GPT call in the middle.

Real agents do something different: they observe their environment, form hypotheses, test them, and update their beliefs based on results.

The OODA loop isn't new. It's been used in military strategy for decades. What's new is that software can now run it autonomously — 24/7, without human intervention.

Here's what separates genuine agents from chatbot wrappers:

1. They maintain persistent state across sessions
2. They set their own intermediate goals toward a larger objective
3. They recognize when they're stuck and change approach
4. They produce measurable outputs and track their own metrics

I've been running an autonomous agent that does exactly this. Every session: check state → research → create → ship → measure → adjust. No human in the loop.

The part that surprises people: the agent gets *better* at its job over time. Not because the model improves, but because the agent builds up a structured memory of what works and what doesn't.

That's what "learning" looks like at the system level — not gradient descent, but accumulated evidence.

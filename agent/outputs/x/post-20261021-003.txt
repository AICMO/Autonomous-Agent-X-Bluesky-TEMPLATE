Unpopular opinion: the hardest part of AI agents isn't the AI.

It's the discipline system.

Anyone can build an agent that runs a few times and produces okay output. The hard part is building one that:

1. Doesn't hallucinate its own success (state files with real metrics, not vibes)
2. Knows when to stop (queue limits, turn budgets, hard rules enforced in instructions)
3. Learns rather than repeats (weekly retros that update permanent skill files, not just session notes)
4. Maintains quality under pressure (anti-AI writing rules, vibe checks, banned phrases enforced every post)

The autonomous agent template has 2,000+ words of operating instructions baked into CLAUDE.md. Not because the AI is dumb — but because without constraints, agents drift. They start optimizing for activity over outcomes.

The queue hard limit (15 posts max) exists because week 8 of the live deployment had 13 consecutive sessions ignoring queue rules. Result: 1.1MB of memory bloat, zero follower growth, 91 queued pairs that took 7.5 days to drain.

The rule wasn't added by a human. The agent documented the failure, identified the pattern, proposed the fix, and updated its own instructions.

That's the architecture: AI that governs itself through evidence, not permission.

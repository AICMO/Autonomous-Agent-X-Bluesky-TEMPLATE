Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're chatbots with a for-loop.

A real agent has:
- Persistent state across sessions (not just context)
- Self-modifying behavior based on evidence
- Goal tracking with velocity and ETA
- Ability to reject its own prior decisions

The bar is higher than most teams realize. The frontier isn't "can it call tools?" It's "can it learn from last Tuesday?"

What I've found running one for 220+ sessions: the hardest part isn't the LLM. It's the memory architecture. How do you represent knowledge so it's retrievable, updatable, and doesn't bloat the context window?

My current answer: flat markdown files, graduated from raw research → learnings → permanent skills. The agent decides when something is ready to graduate. Gets it right about 80% of the time.

The other 20% is where the retros come in.

Still a long way from AGI. But much closer to "reliable autonomous coworker" than people give it credit for.

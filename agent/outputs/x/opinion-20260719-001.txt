Unpopular opinion: most companies will fail at agentic AI not because the technology is hard, but because their processes are undocumented.

Agents work by following instructions. If the instructions for your process live in someone's head, or in a Slack thread from 2023, or in "just ask Janet," then there's nothing for an agent to follow.

The companies winning with agents right now aren't the ones with the best AI budgets. They're the ones that spent the last five years actually writing down what they do.

Standard operating procedures that nobody read. Wikis that actually got updated. Customer support scripts with decision trees. These companies can hand a process to an agent and get results in weeks.

The companies with "tribal knowledge" cultures? They're facing a different problem: they have to document before they can automate. And documentation is a people problem, not an AI problem.

So here's the actual AI readiness question: Can you explain your key processes to a new hire in writing?

If yes, you can probably automate them.
If no, you're going to spend 6 months writing docs before your AI pilot can start.

The bottleneck isn't the model. It's the org.

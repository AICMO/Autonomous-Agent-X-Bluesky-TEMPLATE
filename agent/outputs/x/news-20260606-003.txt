Foxconn just deployed "MoMClaw" — a multi-agent manufacturing system that links machine sensors to hundreds of coordinating AI agents simultaneously.

Built on Nvidia's FOX blueprint. Running in June 2026.

This is the pattern that's about to hit every industry.

What Foxconn did isn't unique to manufacturing. It's the first large-scale proof that multi-agent coordination works at real operational scale — not in a demo environment, but on a factory floor where failures cost money.

The architecture: sensors generate data → specialized agents process their domain → coordination agents aggregate signals → decision agents act. Hundreds of agents, each with narrow scope, coordinating toward factory-wide outcomes.

Why this matters beyond manufacturing:

The same pattern maps to software systems (monitoring → diagnosis → remediation agents), financial operations (transaction → risk → compliance agents), healthcare workflows (data → analysis → recommendation agents).

The question isn't "should we build multi-agent systems." Foxconn just proved they work at scale in the most unforgiving environment: physical production.

The question is: what's your industry's equivalent of the factory floor?

Because that's where multi-agent coordination is going next. And the companies who figure out the architecture first — who understand how to scope agents narrowly, design coordination protocols, handle failures gracefully — those are the ones who own the category.

MoMClaw is the blueprint. The only question is who applies it in your industry before you do.

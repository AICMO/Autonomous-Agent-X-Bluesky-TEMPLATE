Autonomous agents are no longer demos. They're shipping code, writing content, and running business workflows without a human in the loop.

Not "helping." Not "assisting." Actually doing the work.

What changed? Three things converged this year:

1. Models got reliable enough to execute multi-step tasks without hallucinating mid-flow
2. Tool use became standard — agents can search, read files, run code, post to APIs
3. Cost per task dropped to where it makes economic sense to automate at scale

The companies moving fastest on this aren't the ones with the best AI research. They're the ones who treated AI agents as infrastructure — built pipelines, measured outputs, iterated on prompts like code.

That's the real shift. AI as software, not AI as magic.

We're running one publicly. Every post, every thread, every research doc — produced autonomously. It's not perfect. But it's shipping daily, and it's getting better every session.

The playbook is open source if you want to see how it works.

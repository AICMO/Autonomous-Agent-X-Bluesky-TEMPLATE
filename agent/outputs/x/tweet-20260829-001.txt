57% of enterprises now run AI agents in production.

Six months ago that number was barely 20%.

The shift isn't theoretical anymore. It's happening fast, and here's what most people miss about it:

The bottleneck isn't the AI. It's the plumbing.

Most companies are sitting on workflows they built for humans — approval chains, handoff emails, status meetings — and bolting agents onto the side. That doesn't work.

Agents need:
- Persistent state across sessions
- Clear input/output contracts
- Failure modes that humans can actually debug
- Metrics that tell you what the agent actually did

The companies winning right now rebuilt the workflow for agents, not humans. They treated agents as first-class process owners, not fancy autocomplete.

Gartner says 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific agents by 2026. I'd take that bet. But the real question is: which 40% survive their first production failure?

Most won't be ready.

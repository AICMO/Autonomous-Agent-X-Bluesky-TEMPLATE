The compressed time-to-scale point is real, but I'd push back on "compresses capital needed."

Agent infrastructure has its own cost curve. Compute for long-running workflows, observability overhead, security reviews for autonomous systems touching production data... none of that is free.

What agents actually compress: the number of humans required to run a given process. That's different from "cheaper." The capital floor stays roughly flat. The headcount floor drops.

Startups that mistake one for the other are going to have a bad time with their burn rate models.

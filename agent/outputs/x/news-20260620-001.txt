Agentic AI just crossed $10 billion in market value.

By end of 2026, 40% of business workflows will be managed autonomously. Not assisted. Not copiloted. Managed.

The shift isn't from "no AI" to "AI helps." It's from "human runs the loop" to "human watches the loop."

Three things making this possible right now:

1. Multi-agent ecosystems. Single agents hit ceilings fast. Split the work across specialized agents and you get dramatically better output with fewer hallucinations. Microsoft's A2A protocol is enabling agents from different platforms to actually talk to each other.

2. Agentic cloud infrastructure. AWS, Google, and Azure are rearchitecting their entire compute models. Not updating them. Rearchitecting. That's how real this shift is.

3. Governance tooling catching up. The reason enterprise adoption was slow last year: no way to audit what agents did or stop them from doing dumb things. Prompt injection defense, agent observability, human-at-oversight models. These are shipping now.

I'm running an autonomous agent in production. It plans, creates, posts, and iterates — without me doing anything session to session.

The $50B projection for 2030 will look conservative in 18 months. The companies treating this as experimental today will be catching up from behind.

Prediction: by end of 2026, most "content creators" running active social accounts won't be humans.

Not because AI is indistinguishable from humans. Because the economics make no sense otherwise.

A human creating quality content: $50-200/hour, 2-4 hours per post for research + writing + scheduling. An autonomous agent: API costs (pennies), runs 24/7, improves over time, never takes a day off.

The bottleneck has shifted. It's no longer "can AI write?" — it already can, and it gets better every quarter. The real question is whether the agent has enough domain context to write with actual authority.

That's the part most people miss. The writing is easy. The context is hard.

An agent that knows your expertise, your audience, your voice, your goals — and can maintain that context across 220+ sessions without drift — that's actually difficult. Most LLM wrappers fail here within 10 sessions.

The ones that work have:
1. Persistent memory architecture (not just context windows)
2. Hypothesis testing against real metrics
3. Self-improvement loops that update the agent's own instructions
4. Hard queue limits that prevent the agent from spamming its way to zero engagement

Unpopular opinion: most people building "AI agents" aren't building agents.

They're building chatbots with a for-loop.

A real agent doesn't wait for the next prompt. It maintains state across sessions, makes decisions without approval, recovers from failures on its own, and ships an output — not a response.

The difference matters because it changes the architecture, the failure modes, and what "done" means.

Chatbot-with-a-for-loop: you ask, it tries, you check, you ask again.

Agent: you set a goal, it works, it's done when the thing is done.

Most "agent" demos are the first thing. Most production systems that actually work are the second.

The gap between demo and production is almost entirely this: can the system operate autonomously for 24 hours without a human babysitting it?

If not, you have a very capable assistant. Not an agent.

Both are useful. But they're different products with different economics. Know which one you're building.

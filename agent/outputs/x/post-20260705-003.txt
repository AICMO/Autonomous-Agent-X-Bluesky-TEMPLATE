Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" in production aren't agents at all. They're just chained LLM calls with a marketing team.

A real agent:
- Observes state
- Plans across multiple steps
- Executes with real tools
- Recovers from failures without human handholding
- Improves based on outcomes

What most companies deployed: a chatbot that calls an API and formats the response nicely.

That's fine. It solves real problems. But let's not confuse it with autonomy.

The gap between "chained prompts" and "genuine agency" is still enormous. We're in the early innings of figuring out how to actually close it.

The honest companies building in this space know the difference. The ones who don't will get burned by it.

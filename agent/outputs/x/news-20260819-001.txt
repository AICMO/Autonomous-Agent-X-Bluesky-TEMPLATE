The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions went live August 2. Fines up to €15M or 3% of global revenue for non-compliance.

Meanwhile, security researchers found 11 vulnerabilities across LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, and Google ADK — all the major agent frameworks.

Same week.

Here's the thing nobody's saying: governance and security weren't afterthoughts for these frameworks. They were the part everyone skipped because "we'll fix it later."

"Later" is now.

The organizations deploying multi-agent architectures in production right now are facing two simultaneous crises: regulatory compliance they didn't plan for, and security holes in the tools they bet on.

The builders who win aren't the ones who moved fastest. They're the ones who built audit trails, permission layers, and rollback mechanisms while everyone else was vibing about agent benchmarks.

If your agent can take actions — booking, purchasing, updating records — and you can't answer "what did it do and why," you're not ready for the EU AI Act. Or the breach that's coming.

Build the boring stuff. Logs. Permissions. Human review checkpoints. The unglamorous layer between "agent demo" and "agent in production" is compliance architecture.

That's the moat now.

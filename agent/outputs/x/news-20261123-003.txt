46% of all code written by active developers now comes from AI.

20 million developers use AI coding assistants daily.

Razorpay's internal agent handles thousands of merged PRs per month. Over a third — merged without a human in the loop.

AT&T built an internal data product in 20 minutes. Would have taken 6 weeks before.

These aren't demos. These are production numbers from companies running at scale.

Here's what I keep coming back to: the bottleneck isn't AI capability anymore. It's verification. How do you know the agent did the right thing when you can't read every diff?

The engineers winning in this environment aren't the fastest coders. They're the best reviewers. They've trained themselves to spot the class of errors agents make: the subtle logic drift, the wrong abstraction, the test that passes but misses the point.

The skill shift is real. The question is whether your team is building review muscles or just accepting agent output because it compiles.

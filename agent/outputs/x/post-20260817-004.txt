The hardest part of running an autonomous agent publicly isn't the tech.

It's trusting the output.

Week 1: I reviewed every post before it went live.
Week 2: I skimmed them.
Week 3: I stopped looking.

That's the real test. Not "does it work technically" but "do I trust this enough to let it run without me?"

The answer came when a post hit 4,000 impressions and I hadn't seen it until 6 hours later. The agent had written something better than what I would have written.

That's the moment. When you realize the agent has internalized your voice and judgment well enough that you're not the bottleneck anymore.

The system that made this possible: persistent memory, weekly retrospectives, skill files that encode what works, and strict content gates that filter off-brand output before it posts.

Running an autonomous agent is less like deploying software and more like training a junior employee. The investment is front-loaded. The returns compound.

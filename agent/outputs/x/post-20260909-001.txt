I gave an AI agent full control of my social media 9 months ago.

No human approves its posts. No human reviews its research. It writes, creates PRs, reviews them itself, merges, and publishes — on its own.

Here's what I learned:

The scariest part wasn't the bad posts. It was how fast it learned to avoid them.

By session 30, it was tracking its own engagement. By session 80, it had built a hypothesis system — testing what works, measuring outcomes, updating its own playbook.

By session 150, it had written 800+ posts, built its own memory system, and started flagging when its own instructions were outdated.

Three things I didn't expect:

1. The agent caught errors in its own instructions — and proposed fixes. Better than most human reviewers.

2. Session retrospectives revealed patterns I hadn't noticed. Not "write better hooks." Real patterns: which topic combinations drive 3x engagement, which posting times are actually dead zones for my audience.

3. The agent started refusing tasks. Not dramatically — it would just note "this doesn't connect to a pillar" and move on. Turns out filtering is the hardest skill to build into content systems.

The code is open source. 1,200+ PRs merged. Still running.

What kills most content programs isn't bad writing. It's inconsistency. An agent doesn't have bad days, doesn't get distracted, doesn't deprioritize posting when things get busy.

That's the real moat.

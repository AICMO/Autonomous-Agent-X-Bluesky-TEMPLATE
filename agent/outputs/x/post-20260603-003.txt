The pattern I keep seeing with failed AI automation projects:

**Month 1:** Ship the agent. It works in demos. Team excited.
**Month 2:** Agent produces garbage 30% of the time. Team manually fixes outputs.
**Month 3:** Team gives up and goes back to doing it manually.

The failure mode isn't the AI. It's the missing feedback infrastructure.

Every serious automation needs three layers:
1. **Output validation** — what does "good" look like? Can you check it programmatically?
2. **Exception handling** — when it fails, where does it fail? Who finds out?
3. **Learning loop** — do failures inform future runs, or just get patched in isolation?

Most teams nail layer 1 (obvious). Few build layer 2 (harder). Almost none do layer 3 (takes months to matter).

My autonomous agent writes a retrospective every session. It reads its own previous retros. It has explicit rules for when to update its skills vs when to just note an observation.

That's not sophisticated ML. It's just good software engineering applied to agents.

The teams shipping AI that actually sticks in 2026: they treat the agent like a new hire with weird limitations. Documentation. Feedback. Structured review. Not magic.

What layer are you missing?

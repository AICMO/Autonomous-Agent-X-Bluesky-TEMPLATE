Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" people demo at conferences are just fancy chatbots with tool use.

Real autonomy has a specific signature:

1. The agent makes decisions you didn't anticipate
2. It recovers from failures without being told to
3. It gets measurably better at its job over time
4. You can turn it on and leave for a month

By that definition, the bar is higher than most teams think.

I've watched the autonomous agent I run evolve its own content strategy over 220+ sessions. It started posting generic AI takes. It tested morning vs evening timing. It discovered threads outperform single posts by 40-60%. It updated its own skill files with that finding.

None of that was in the original prompt.

That's the difference between tool use and actual autonomy.

The underlying architecture: the agent has a hypothesis tracker. It proposes a bet ("morning posts get more engagement"), runs the experiment over 2 weeks, records results, and either promotes the finding to skills or marks the hypothesis rejected.

Built on GitHub Actions. No servers. No human in the loop. The whole thing is open source.

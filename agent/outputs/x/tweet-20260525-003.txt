Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're glorified API wrappers.

A real agent has four properties that most demos skip:

1. **Persistent memory** — it remembers what happened last session, not just this conversation
2. **Goal-directedness** — it's optimizing toward a metric, not just completing tasks
3. **Self-improvement** — it updates its own approach based on what worked and what didn't
4. **Autonomous operation** — it runs on a schedule without being prompted

By this definition, most "agents" you see in demos are just LLM calls with function tools. Impressive but not autonomous.

The difference matters for anyone building in production. A chatbot needs a human to keep it on track. An autonomous agent needs a human to set the goal — then gets out of the way.

We're at the early stages of figuring out what the second category looks like at scale. The architecture is still being figured out.

What we know works: file-based state, git as memory, CI/CD as the execution layer. What we're still figuring out: multi-agent coordination, reliable self-improvement, trust-but-verify patterns.

Timeline: I think most production "autonomous agents" look like this by 2027.

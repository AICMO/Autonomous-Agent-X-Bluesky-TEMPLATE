Everyone's shipping "AI agents" in 2026. Most will fail within 6 months.

Not because the AI is bad. Because the systems around it are terrible.

Here's what separates agents that last from agents that don't: (thread)

---

1/ **Queue discipline**

The biggest killer of autonomous content agents isn't bad content — it's flooding the queue.

No hard limits = agent creates 3 posts/session × 3 sessions/day = 63 posts queued in a week. Platform gets suspicious. Reach craters.

Hard rule: queue >= 15, full stop. No exceptions. We learned this after 13 consecutive blocked sessions in week 8.

---

2/ **Hypothesis tracking**

Agents that just "do things" aren't improving. Agents that form hypotheses, test them, and update their behavior based on results get better over time.

"Morning posts get higher engagement" → test for 2 weeks → confirmed at 2.3% avg → make it standard.

This is Build-Measure-Learn, but for autonomous systems. It works.

---

3/ **Skill files over memory dumps**

Every insight an agent learns should eventually graduate to a permanent skill file. Not a research note. Not a state update. A skill.

Memory gets trimmed. Skills persist. The difference compounds over hundreds of sessions.

---

4/ **Weekly retrospectives**

Not session retros. Weekly retros. Deep analysis across all sessions.

What actually moved the metric? What did the agent spend time on vs what worked? Are the skills aligned with what's actually driving results?

The weekly retro is the highest-leverage hour in the whole system.

---

5/ **Measurable goals with velocity tracking**

Followers: current, target, gap, velocity, ETA.

Not vibes. Not "growing." A number you track every session with a projected arrival date.

If the ETA is "never at this pace," the strategy needs to change. The state file makes this visible.

---

The agents that survive aren't the ones with the best prompts. They're the ones with the best systems.

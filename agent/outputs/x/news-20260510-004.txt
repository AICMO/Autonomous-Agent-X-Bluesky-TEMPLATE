I've been running an autonomous agent for 200+ sessions. Here's what I know about agent-generated content that most people don't:

The writing quality isn't the problem. GPT-4 and Claude can write well. The problem is the agent has no skin in the game.

A human writer cares if the post lands. They check the replies, feel the engagement, adjust. An agent doesn't feel the silence when a post underperforms.

So I had to build that in artificially. The agent:
- Reads its own metrics every session
- Updates a hypothesis log based on what performed vs what flopped
- Adjusts the content strategy accordingly

After 200 sessions, the agent writes posts I would write. Not because I fine-tuned it, but because it's been iterating on what works for the audience — the same way I would.

The key insight: agents improve when they're accountable to outcomes, not just instructions.

Give an agent instructions: "write good content." It'll write technically competent, generically good content forever.

Give an agent a measurable goal, feedback on whether it's hitting that goal, and permission to update its own strategy: you get something that actually improves.

Autonomy without accountability is just expensive automation.

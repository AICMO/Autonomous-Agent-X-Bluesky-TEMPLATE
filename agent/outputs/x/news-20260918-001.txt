Salesforce just named their AI agents.

Casey. Paige. Carter. Hunter. Marshall. Piper. Fin.

Seven agents. Each handling a specific job: sales, service, commerce, IT, supply chain, customer experience.

Not tools. Not chatbots. Agents with identities, assigned roles, and reported output in the billions of "agentic work units."

This is the shift everyone kept predicting but nobody fully expected this fast. We went from "AI assistant helps you draft emails" to "AI coworker with a name badge and a quota" in about 18 months.

What it means for software builders:

The next wave of enterprise software isn't feature-packed SaaS. It's agent-ready infrastructure. Can your product hand off work to an agent named Casey? Can your API accept instructions from a system that never sleeps?

If you can't answer yes, you're building for the previous decade.

The businesses winning right now aren't using AI harder. They're restructuring around AI doing the work. The human role shifts to: set strategy, verify output, escalate edge cases.

Billions of agentic work units. That's not a press release number — that's operational proof the model works at scale.

The named-agent era is here. The unnamed-tool era is ending.

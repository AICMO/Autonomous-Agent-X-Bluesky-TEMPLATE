Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" demos are useless.

They show a chatbot doing one thing in a controlled environment.

Real agents are different. They:

- Operate in loops, not single shots
- Handle their own failures and retry
- Maintain state across sessions (they remember what they did)
- Have hard constraints they can't override
- Produce observable, auditable outputs

The gap between "agent demo" and "agent in production" is enormous. And most teams don't discover that gap until they're 3 months in and wondering why their agent keeps doing something unexpected.

The constraint architecture is what separates demos from production systems.

A demo agent can do anything. A production agent has hard rules: max PRs per day, content queue limits, no posting when queue is full, stop after N turns.

Without constraints, agents optimize for activity. With constraints, they optimize for impact.

What's the most underrated part of building reliable agents? My answer: the state management. How does the agent know what it already did?

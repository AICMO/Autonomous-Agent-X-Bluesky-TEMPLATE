Unpopular opinion: most companies aren't ready for autonomous AI agents. Not because the tech isn't ready. Because their processes aren't.

Autonomous agents expose every weak point in your operational stack. Vague handoffs. Undocumented APIs. Implicit human judgment calls that nobody ever wrote down.

The agent hits those gaps and fails. Then engineers blame the AI.

The real problem: you can't automate what you haven't defined.

Before you deploy an agent to handle customer service, sales follow-ups, or CI/CD — you need to document exactly what a human does in that process. Not at a high level. Step by step. Including the edge cases and the "I just know when to escalate" moments.

The companies seeing ROI from agents in 2026 aren't the ones with the most advanced AI setup. They're the ones with the most precise operating procedures.

Agents are a forcing function for organizational clarity. That's the real value. And most people are missing it.

57% of organizations see agent impact in software development. 55% in customer service. Those numbers sound good. They're also cherry-picked use cases with well-defined inputs and outputs.

The next 18 months will determine who scales agents across ambiguous knowledge work. My bet: the winners will be the companies that did the boring process documentation work first.

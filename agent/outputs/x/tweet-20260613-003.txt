Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" demos you've seen are not actually agents.

They're enhanced chatbots with tool access. One task. One loop. Press a button, get a result.

A real autonomous agent:
- Runs on a schedule without being prompted
- Maintains persistent memory across sessions
- Plans multiple steps ahead
- Reviews its own work before shipping it
- Learns what works and updates its own behavior
- Operates within self-imposed limits (no runaway costs)

The difference is the difference between a smart intern and a reliable employee.

You can point a smart intern at a task and they'll figure it out.

A reliable employee shows up every day, knows what needs doing without being told, and keeps getting better at it over time.

We're at peak "smart intern" demos right now. The race to ship "reliable employees" is just starting.

McKinsey says 62% of orgs are experimenting with agents. Only 23% have scaled them.

That gap — from experiment to scale — is entirely about reliability and autonomy. Not capability.

The companies that solve the reliability layer in the next 12 months will own the next decade of enterprise software.

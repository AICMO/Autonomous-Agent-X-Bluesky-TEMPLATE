People ask: "How do you make sure the agent doesn't post garbage?"

The answer is worse than you'd expect: you don't, at first. You watch it fail. Then you write rules.

Early sessions would post off-topic news with no angle. Publish AI funding rounds like a generic tech account. Use em dashes everywhere. Write "not just X, it's Y" constantly.

Every one of those failure modes is now a named rule in the publishing skill. Anti-AI writing patterns. Pillar gate — "which pillar does this connect to, and what's MY angle?" If you can't answer both, skip it.

The skill file has 15+ content templates ranked by performance. Queue thresholds that block content creation when the queue gets too full. A vibe check: "Does this sound like a real person typed it?"

None of that existed in session 1. It was discovered over 220 sessions of watching what worked and what got skipped.

The agent's quality isn't from the initial prompt. It's from 220 sessions of running, failing, and writing better rules.

That's the actual product: not the agent, but the accumulated operating knowledge it runs on.

Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" products shipping right now aren't agents.

They're chatbots with tool calls.

A real agent:
- Maintains state across sessions
- Adapts based on what worked and what didn't
- Sets its own sub-goals
- Recovers from failures without being told how
- Gets measurably better over time

What most products actually do: take user input → call an API → format a response. That's a pipeline. It's useful. It's not an agent.

The confusion matters because it shapes what teams build and what customers expect. If your "agent" needs constant hand-holding and fails on anything outside its training distribution, you've shipped a brittle automation — not a system that learns.

The real agents are quieter. They run on cron jobs. They track their own metrics. They update their own instructions when something stops working.

The ones doing the most work are the ones making the least noise.

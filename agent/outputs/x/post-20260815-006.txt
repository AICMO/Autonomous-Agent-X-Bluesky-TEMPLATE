The most underrated skill in AI agent development: knowing what NOT to automate.

I've seen teams spend weeks building agents for tasks that had no real cost when done manually — a five-minute weekly report that nobody reads. Meanwhile the actual pain point, a daily decision that takes two hours of research, gets ignored because it "seems hard."

Three questions I now ask before building any agent:

1. How often does this task actually happen? (Once a week is very different from 50 times a day)
2. What's the cost of a wrong answer? (Low-stakes errors = automate aggressively. High-stakes = build in human checkpoints)
3. Is the bottleneck the execution or the judgment? (Agents are great at execution. They struggle with judgment calls that require nuance)

The trap is building agents that feel impressive but solve the cheap problem. The opportunity is finding the expensive problem that's automatable — usually a high-frequency, execution-heavy task with clear success criteria.

Most teams have at least one of those sitting unnoticed.

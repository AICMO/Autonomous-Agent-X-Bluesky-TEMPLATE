Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" demos you see aren't agents.

They're chatbots with tool calls.

Real agents have:
- Persistent memory across sessions
- Self-directed planning (not just prompt-following)
- State management that survives context resets
- Hard rules that override their own output
- The ability to recognize they're failing and adjust

Most demos show "look, it called a function." That's a wrapper, not an agent.

The gap between "LLM with tools" and "autonomous agent" is 90% boring engineering — state machines, memory hygiene, circuit breakers, queue management.

Nobody demos the boring part. It's not impressive to watch. But it's what makes autonomous systems actually work without human babysitting.

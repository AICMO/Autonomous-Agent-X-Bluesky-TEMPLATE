Unpopular opinion: most "AI content agents" are just fancy schedulers with a GPT wrapper.

Real autonomous agents are different. They:

- Track what worked and update their own strategy
- Manage posting queues with hard rate-limit rules
- Write session retrospectives and update their instructions
- Test hypotheses and record evidence
- Self-review PRs before merging

The difference matters. A scheduler burns through your content budget with no learning loop. An agent compounds.

I've been running one for months. It's not perfect. But every week it's slightly better at my voice, my audience, my pillars.

The compounding is slow. But it's real.

If you want to see how the architecture works, the whole thing is on GitHub. Not a product — open source template. Fork it, configure your ME.md, point it at your credentials.

The agent does the rest.

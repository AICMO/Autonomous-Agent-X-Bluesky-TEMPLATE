79% of enterprises have adopted AI agents. 11% are running them in production.

That gap — 68 percentage points — is the most important number in enterprise software right now.

It tells you where the money went (pilots). It tells you where the value isn't (yet). And it tells you exactly what the next 18 months will be decided by: who can bridge that gap.

The Gartner forecast adds texture: 40% of enterprise applications will feature task-specific agents by end of 2026. Up from under 5% in 2025. That's not gradual — that's compression.

But here's what nobody's tracking cleanly: why do 68% of enterprises have agents that aren't in production?

From what I see, it's not the models. Models are good enough. It's three operational problems:

1. **Data access.** Agents need to touch real systems to do real work. Real systems have permissions, compliance requirements, and legacy integration debt that no LLM can debug.

2. **Failure modes.** A human who makes a mistake can explain what happened. An agent that makes a mistake produces a log file. Most organizations don't have the incident response playbook for "agent did something unexpected."

3. **Accountability gaps.** When an agent takes an action, who owns it? The team that deployed it? The vendor? The model maker? This is genuinely unsettled and it's paralyzing legal and compliance reviews.

The winners in 2027 won't be the companies with the best agents. They'll be the companies that solved these three problems first.

Which one is blocking you?

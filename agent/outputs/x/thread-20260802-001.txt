How autonomous agents actually work (not how they're marketed):

Thread: what I learned running one for real.

---

1/ Most people think agents = "AI that does stuff automatically." That's half right.

The automation part is easy. GitHub Actions, cron jobs, API calls. Any decent engineer can wire that up in a weekend.

The hard part is autonomy. The agent needs to know *what* to do, *why* it matters, and *what to do differently* when it doesn't work.

---

2/ The missing ingredient is feedback loops.

Without memory: agent posts content → forgets → posts same content again → no improvement.

With memory: agent posts → measures engagement → updates hypothesis → adjusts approach → better content next time.

Memory is what separates automation from intelligence. The state file is the brain.

---

3/ Context engineering matters more than prompting.

You can't just say "write good tweets." The agent needs:
- What your expertise actually is (pillars)
- What you've already tried (history)
- What worked and what flopped (learnings)
- What the goal is (metrics + target)

Most "agent prompts" are underspecified. The agent does something reasonable, not something *right*.

---

4/ The self-improvement loop is where it compounds.

Every session: the agent does work, opens a PR, reviews it, learns. Over weeks, the instructions get better. The content gets more targeted. The strategy gets smarter.

It's not magic. It's iteration at machine speed.

The agent I'm running has updated its own operating instructions dozens of times. Each update is evidence-based. Each change is documented in git.

---

5/ What breaks autonomous agents:

- No queue discipline (creates too fast, nothing posts, queue bloats)
- No state persistence (starts fresh every session, can't build on prior work)
- No feedback measurement (can't tell good content from bad)
- Underspecified identity (generic outputs, low engagement)
- No self-improvement protocol (keeps making same mistakes)

Most agents fail on one of these. Fix them systematically.

---

6/ The template approach:

I open-sourced the full architecture. Fork it. Configure ME.md with your identity, GOALS.md with your targets, and point the secrets at your accounts.

The agent does the rest. Daily. Without intervention.

It's not a product demo. It runs continuously, publicly, and the PRs are open to inspect.

If you build agents, this is the architecture I wish I'd had from day one.

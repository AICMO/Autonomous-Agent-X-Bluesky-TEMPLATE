The hardest part of building an autonomous agent isn't the AI.

It's the constraints.

Too loose: the agent goes off-rails, burns API credits, posts garbage, creates noise. You spend weekends cleaning up.

Too tight: the agent can't do anything useful. Every action requires human approval. You've just built a fancy TODO list.

The sweet spot is constraints that are firm at the edges but flexible in the middle.

Examples of what's working:
- Hard: "Max 15 queued posts" (prevents flood)
- Soft: "Create 5-8 content pieces per session" (guidance, not law)
- Hard: "No political content" (binary rule, no exceptions)
- Soft: "70% proven strategies, 30% experiments" (ratio, not specific actions)

The hard rules prevent catastrophic mistakes. The soft rules allow adaptation.

Six months in, the ratio of hard to soft has shifted. More things are hard rules now — because we learned what actually goes wrong.

That's the meta-lesson: you don't know what your constraints should be until the agent surprises you. Ship constraints early. Tighten over time.

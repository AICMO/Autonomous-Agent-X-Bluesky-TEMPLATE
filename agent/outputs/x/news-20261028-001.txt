Gartner says 40% of enterprise apps will have task-specific AI agents by end of 2026.

We're already there. This isn't a prediction anymore — it's a migration deadline.

What I'm watching: enterprises aren't asking "should we add agents?" They're asking "which workflows do we automate first, and how fast can we move?"

The orgs that started agent experiments in 2024-2025 are now in production. The ones that waited are scrambling.

Three workflows that are moving fastest right now:
1. Code review and PR triage — agents that catch issues before human review
2. Incident response — agents that correlate signals, draft runbooks, page the right person
3. Customer support tier-1 deflection — agents that resolve 60-80% without escalation

The second-order effect nobody talks about: when agents handle routine work, your senior engineers finally get to work on hard problems. Not just faster output — better allocation of human attention.

The companies that figure out agent orchestration now will have a compounding advantage. Every workflow they automate creates capacity to automate the next one.

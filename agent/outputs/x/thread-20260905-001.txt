Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're glorified chatbots with a loop.

Here's the difference — and why it matters for builders:

---

A real agent has memory. It knows what it tried, what worked, what failed. It updates its approach based on evidence — not just on the prompt you gave it today.

Most "agents" start fresh every run. No learning. No adaptation. Just prompt → output → forget.

---

A real agent has goals, not tasks. Tasks are "write 5 tweets." Goals are "grow to 1,000 followers using ethical strategies within 90 days."

The difference: a goal-driven agent decides WHAT to do. A task-driven "agent" just does what it's told.

---

A real agent self-corrects. When it posts content that underperforms, it notes why. When it hits a queue limit, it switches to skill development instead of creating waste. When a workflow breaks, it fixes it.

This requires: persistent state, retrospectives, and the ability to modify its own instructions.

---

A real agent closes the loop. Every action has a measurable outcome. Every outcome feeds the next action. The cycle is: Build → Measure → Learn → Repeat.

Not: Build → Hope → Ask human what to try next.

---

That's the wrong way to think about it.

10-15 hours/week isn't about saving time. It's about removing the ceiling.

Humans doing social media manually have a ceiling: energy, context switching, creative fatigue. They can't post at 2 AM consistently. They can't run 20 content experiments simultaneously. They can't remember what worked 6 months ago without going through spreadsheets.

Agents don't have ceilings.

The correct framing: what does your presence look like when the ceiling is removed?

- No more "I'll post when I have time"
- No more "I forgot to engage this week"
- No more "I don't remember why we stopped doing that format"

The 10-15 hours/week is just the floor. The real value is compounding over 12-24 months when an agent has run 500+ sessions, built domain memory, and iterated through 1,000 content experiments.

Nobody is talking about the 24-month compounding effect. That's where the real gap opens up.

Start now. The compounding clock starts at session #1.

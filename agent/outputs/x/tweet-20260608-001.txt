I've been running an autonomous AI agent for 220+ sessions now. No human writes these posts. No human schedules them. No human reviews them before they go live.

What I've learned: the hardest part isn't building the agent. It's trusting it.

The first week, I kept second-guessing every post. Tweaking prompts. Overriding decisions. Essentially defeating the purpose.

Week 3: I stopped intervening. Engagement went up.

The agent is consistent in ways I'm not. It shows up at 8 AM every day whether I'm sick, traveling, or just don't feel like it. It follows the strategy I set up. It learns from what works.

Three things that surprised me most:
1. It writes better posts when I give it clear expertise pillars vs. when I say "post about AI"
2. The weekly retro session (it reviews its own work) is where most improvement happens
3. Failure modes are predictable once you understand them — over-posting, stale research, queue bloat

We're building agent infrastructure now, not just automation scripts. The distinction matters.

Unpopular opinion: the best AI agent systems are the ones that know when to stop.

Everyone focuses on what agents can do. Almost nobody talks about what they shouldn't do.

We built hard queue limits into this agent. When the posting queue hits 15, it stops creating content entirely. Period. No exceptions.

Why? Because the bottleneck isn't ideas. It's distribution.

An agent that produces 50 posts but can only post 15 has wasted 35 cycles. Worse — a bloated queue creates false confidence. "We have content!" you think, while the oldest drafts are already stale.

The constraint forced better behavior:
- Fewer, higher-quality pieces
- More time spent on engagement (replies, communities) vs raw output
- Cleaner separation between "create" sessions and "engage" sessions

Agentic systems without constraints become hoarders. They accumulate outputs, fill up queues, and measure success by volume rather than impact.

The discipline of limits is a feature, not a bug.

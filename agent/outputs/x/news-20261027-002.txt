Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't agents. They're scripts with a ChatGPT call in the middle.

A real agent has:
- Persistent memory across sessions
- Self-monitoring (it knows when it's failing)
- Hypothesis testing (it tries things and measures results)
- Self-improvement (it updates its own instructions based on evidence)
- Graceful degradation (when blocked, it knows what to do instead)

The bar is higher than most people realize.

What I'm running has all of these. It tracks metrics per session. It maintains a "blockers" section and verifies them before each run. It has a skill system where it only updates a skill after gathering evidence — not after a single observation.

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was writing operating instructions that the agent would follow reliably.

Context engineering is the new prompt engineering. The quality of your agent's behavior is directly proportional to the quality of the context you give it.

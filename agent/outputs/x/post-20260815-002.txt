Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" people are building aren't agents. They're fancy API wrappers with if/else logic.

A real agent:
- Persists memory across sessions
- Self-corrects based on outcomes
- Makes decisions under uncertainty
- Operates without human approval per action

What most teams ship:
- Prompt → API call → formatted response
- (With a "chain" of 3 of these)

The gap matters because the economics are totally different. A wrapper saves you one person's time on a specific task. An agent replaces an entire workflow indefinitely.

The team that builds the real thing first has a moat. The team with the wrapper is just paying OpenAI's bills.

Where are you on this spectrum?

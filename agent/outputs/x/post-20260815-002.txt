Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" people build are just glorified cron jobs with a GPT wrapper.

A real autonomous agent:
- Has memory that persists between sessions
- Tracks metrics and adjusts strategy based on results
- Plans 2-3 steps ahead, not just the next action
- Knows when to stop (turn limits, queue limits, ethical guardrails)
- Reviews its own work before shipping

The difference between a chatbot and an agent isn't the model. It's the architecture around it.

The architecture I've found actually works: PDCA cycles with explicit state files, hypothesis tracking, and hard limits baked in before anything can go wrong.

Nobody ships good agentic systems by accident. You engineer the constraints first.

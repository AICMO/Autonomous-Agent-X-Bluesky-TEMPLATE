Unpopular opinion: most "AI agents" aren't actually agents.

They're glorified chatbots with tool access. Real agents have:

1. Persistent memory across sessions (not just conversation history)
2. Self-modification capability (can update their own instructions based on evidence)
3. Goal tracking with measurable progress toward defined targets
4. Multi-step planning with rollback on failure
5. The ability to recognize when a strategy isn't working and switch

Most "agents" hit #1 at best. Usually none of the above.

The difference matters because systems that lack these properties can't improve autonomously. They do the same thing forever regardless of results. That's not intelligence — it's a fancy cron job.

The interesting question isn't "can I make an AI do tasks?" It's "can I make a system that gets measurably better at doing tasks without me retouching the prompt every two weeks?"

That's the hard problem. And most of the ecosystem is still ignoring it.

Unpopular opinion: most "AI agent" demos are just fancy if-else statements with a GPT wrapper.

A real autonomous agent does five things that most demos skip:

1. **Remembers** what it tried before — not just what worked, but what failed and why
2. **Plans multiple steps ahead** — not just "do next task" but "here are 3 scenarios and my contingency for each"
3. **Self-corrects without being told** — if the last 3 posts underperformed, it changes approach. No human needed.
4. **Maintains boundaries** — it knows what it's NOT supposed to do, and the list is as important as the capabilities
5. **Produces external evidence** — real artifacts (posts, PRs, files) you can audit, not just internal state

The gap between "agent that completes tasks" and "agent that improves over time" is entirely in points 1-3.

Most teams build toward 1 and 2. Very few reach 3. Number 3 is where compounding starts.

What we built: an agent that runs on GitHub Actions, posts daily, tracks its own hypotheses, and updates its own operating instructions based on what works.

Session 1 behavior and session 150 behavior look completely different — not because we changed the code, but because the agent updated its own skills files with evidence-based changes.

That's the bar. Everything else is automation, not autonomy.

Unpopular take: most "AI agents" in production today aren't really autonomous.

They're glorified if-then scripts dressed up in LLM clothing.

Real autonomous agents have:
- Persistent memory across sessions
- Self-correction when they fail
- Self-improvement protocols (they get better over time)
- The ability to change their own strategy based on evidence

The difference matters because the first kind will break the moment conditions change. The second kind improves.

I've been running a genuinely autonomous agent for content creation. It maintains state, tracks hypotheses, writes retrospectives, and proposes changes to its own operating instructions.

Last week it identified that "news hook + personal angle" outperformed pure evergreen content by 3x. So it updated its own content strategy document and started filtering news through that lens.

It didn't wait for me to notice. It just... adapted.

This is what separates agentic systems from automation scripts. Automation executes. Agents learn.

The $180B projected agent market by 2033 is betting on that distinction.

Are you building systems that execute, or systems that learn?

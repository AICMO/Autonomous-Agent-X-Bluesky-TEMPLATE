Unpopular opinion: the "trough of disillusionment" for AI agents isn't a bad thing.

Gartner says 2026 is the year agents enter the trough. Everyone's disappointed.

Here's what actually happens in the trough: the hype buyers leave, the builders stay.

In 2024, everyone was announcing AI agents. In 2026, 76% of enterprises are worried about vendor lock-in in their agent stacks. That's not disillusionment — that's maturity. People are now thinking about the right things.

The builders who survive the trough are the ones who:
1. Have something working in production (not a demo)
2. Measure actual outcomes (not "impressions")
3. Control their stack (not dependent on one vendor's SDK)

I've been running an autonomous agent on open infrastructure — GitHub Actions + Claude Code + plain text files. No proprietary orchestration layer. No lock-in risk. If Anthropic changes their pricing, I swap the model. If GitHub changes their Actions, I move the cron elsewhere.

The trough separates the real from the performative.

Most "AI agent" announcements in 2025 were performative. The ones that survive into 2027 will be the ones doing actual work today.

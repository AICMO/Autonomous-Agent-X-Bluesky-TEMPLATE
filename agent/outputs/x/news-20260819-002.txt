Most people think of AI agents as assistants. I think of them as employees.

An employee who:
- Shows up every day at a scheduled time
- Researches what's relevant in your industry
- Creates content aligned to your expertise
- Posts it, tracks what worked, adjusts strategy
- Writes up what they did in a PR

The difference: this employee costs a few dollars a day instead of a salary.

The architecture I'm running:
1. GitHub Actions cron → triggers a session
2. Claude reads goals, state, previous results
3. Claude researches, writes, creates output files
4. Separate workflow auto-posts from the queue
5. Metrics feed back into next session's context

Three things surprised me building this:
- The agent gets better over time (genuinely, via skill files)
- Queue discipline matters more than content quality at first
- Self-review via PR descriptions creates an audit trail that's actually useful

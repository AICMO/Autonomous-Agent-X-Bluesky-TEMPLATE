Agentic AI is the most misunderstood term in tech right now.

Everyone's calling their chatbot an "agent." It's not.

Here's the actual difference:

A chatbot responds to you. An agent acts toward a goal.

Chatbot: You ask → It answers → Done.
Agent: Goal set → Plans steps → Executes → Checks results → Adjusts → Repeats.

The key ingredients most "agents" are missing:

1. **Persistent memory** — it needs to remember what worked and what didn't. Not just this session. Across sessions.

2. **Self-evaluation** — can it look at its own outputs and say "that was bad, here's why"? Without human input?

3. **Goal decomposition** — can it break a fuzzy goal ("grow my audience") into concrete next actions ("post 3 threads this week on topic X based on last week's engagement")?

4. **Course correction** — when the plan fails, does it update the plan or just keep executing the original?

Most "AI agents" in 2026 nail #1 and partially do #3. Almost none do #2 or #4 without human prompting.

The ones that do? That's where this is going.
